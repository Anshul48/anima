## Objective

Turn the curated material into a coherent, substantive **proposed architecture** and a credible route to realizing it.

The central ambition is a computational environment in which useful organization can develop: worlds hosting other worlds; entities with persistent state; selective and revisable boundaries; relationships that can acquire specialized ways of cooperating; and organizational changes that can persist, compose and become reusable.

The ambition includes co-adaptation, changes to participating entities, composites and possible assimilation, rather than only plugins, delegation or stateful adapters. The first implementation may support a bounded subset, but the architecture must explain how that subset grows toward the broader ambition and which extensions would actually require changes.

Use STC as the first demanding construction-world use case. Existing TL, TRACE, SST, STC and other projects retain their own delivery tracks. ANIMA development must not become a universal prerequisite for their useful progress.

Produce both:

1. An overarching architecture that accommodates development across software, cognition, cooperating experts and eventually physical systems.
2. A first realizable system with concrete mechanisms, bounded scope, meaningful domain value and discriminating evidence.

Do not make every interesting idea a global requirement. The user seeks a substantial capability advance; proposing more orchestration, more registries or more terminology is insufficient without explaining the change in what the system can accomplish.

## Design work

Choose and defend a recommended architecture. Discuss alternatives where they change consequential behavior, cost or future options. Do not deliver an unranked catalogue of approaches.

### 1. Define the world and its responsibilities

Explain what a world can contain and what remains its responsibility: domain state, goals, representations, tools, resources, obligations, interpretation of outcomes and exported capabilities.

Distinguish identity, containment, ownership, state custody, access, authority, resource allocation and execution location. A folder is not a runtime world. A single ownership tree need not describe overlapping functional networks or coalitions.

Specify how nested worlds and overlapping composites work, how obligations cross boundaries and how a participant retains or transfers responsibilities. Avoid treating every expert as merely a cluster of stored text.

Keep project roles faithful to current contracts. TL's evidence organization and retrieval scope, TRACE's semantic responsibilities, SST's search responsibilities, STC's construction role and the broader host substrate must not be silently merged. ANIMA's source organization does not by itself select a runtime host. The broad substrate is distinct from STC's package named substrate.

### 2. Establish the smallest stable computational floor

Propose the minimal shared machinery for identity, state persistence, communication, capabilities, lifecycle, resources and observable changes. Explain which guarantees it enforces, which are policies that can vary by world and which remain domain responsibilities.

Show enough data structures, operations, pseudocode or state transitions to make the mechanism assessable. A diagram with “learning” or “integration” boxes is not a mechanism.

Keep the floor small enough to avoid repeated framework rewrites. Explain how it can extend or migrate when an innovation exceeds its current primitives; do not claim that every future idea will fit without changes.

### 3. Make the boundaries and relationships genuinely plastic

Give a concrete model of relationship state: participating identities, protocol or procedure, representation mappings, applicability, shared state, resource use, interaction evidence and revision history, as appropriate.

Explain what a relationship can actually change: routing, permeability, translation, delegated work, coordinated execution, shared representations, participating entities or their organization. State which changes need consent or authority from which participant.

A learnable semantic interface still needs executable communication and compatibility rules. Distinguish that technical floor from fixed domain APIs. Show how a new protocol or representation is introduced, evaluated, adopted and superseded without pretending that arbitrary natural language is a reliable universal contract.

Describe how evidence from continued interaction modifies the arrangement. Identify the learner, its observations, its permitted actions, the retained artifact and the effect on subsequent behavior. Adaptation may use existing frozen models, search, learned policies or other mechanisms; changing model weights is not assumed.

### 4. Support organizational development

Work through temporary coalitions, persistent composites, deeper assimilation and dissolution or fission. Explain the differences between wrapping two unchanged participants and developing a new organization with changed state, procedures, responsibilities or capabilities.

For the first supported transformations, specify preconditions, authority, transition steps, continuity, resource accounting and observable outcomes. Address interrupted changes, incompatible representations, failed cooperation and incomplete reversibility.

Allow future co-adaptation of participants rather than permanently restricting plasticity to edges. Separate within-lifetime learning from portable inheritance. A successor might inherit parameters, procedures, relationships, developmental rules or an initial configuration; justify what is transmitted and what must be adapted anew.

Population evolution, open-ended selection and RSI machinery can remain later work. Provide extension paths where they matter, without making them prerequisites for the first useful system or claiming that selection guarantees intelligence.

### 5. Put memory, constraints and resources in the right places

Account for scarce active organization, working state, retained competence, evidence and historical material. Hot, warm and cold layers may each contain several sublayers and edge types; do not impose one storage hierarchy on every world.

Respect the user's direction that active graph space is costly and contested. Retired content must not remain as graph bodies merely to satisfy history queries. Any lightweight pointer, dependency exception or retained note requires a stated purpose and cost. Historical retrieval and reconstruction pay their actual costs.

Do not promise unlimited immutable history or permanent citation resolution from TL: availability after reclamation follows the actual owning retention contract. Recovering raw bytes is different from recovering former identities, relationships or support assessments.

Explain how selective context and constraint enforcement interact: what is enforced structurally, what is a revisable belief, what needs evidence and what cannot be guaranteed by a model. Preserve alternative formulations and unusual evidence where justified; schemas and signposts must not erase the only route to a useful outlier.

Include compute, storage, communication, acquisition, maintenance, background organization and human attention in the account. The system should learn when adaptation is worthwhile and when escalation is appropriate, rather than always adapting or always stopping.

### 6. Demonstrate behavior through worked scenarios

Use a small set of representative journeys, rather than an exhaustive module roster. Include:

- STC doing useful construction work across changed requirements or sessions, developing a reusable contribution from experience, then using it in later work.
- Independently useful participants acquiring a better way of cooperating, including a change to shared representation or participating procedures and a failure or dissolution case.
- A composite or descendant retaining useful competence while encountering a changed environment.
- A future physical construction case that tests whether the architecture can accommodate measured specimens, uncertain physical state, calibration and non-software evidence. Keep this a design stress test, not a requirement to build a humanoid now.

For each, trace state, messages, authority, evidence, costs and failure behavior across boundaries. Identify which steps are available from existing components, which require new machinery and which remain hypotheses. Include at least one situation in which the system should retain its current organization or request a consequential decision instead of changing.

### 7. Select the first realization and its evidence

Recommend one bounded vertical slice based on existing usable primitives. It must provide real domain value and exercise a meaningful developmental change. Registry creation, replayed messages or an impressive generated diagram alone do not qualify.

Define the claim to be tested, the simpler explanation that could account for improvement and the observations that would discriminate them. Compare against a competent simpler arrangement with the same models, tools, memory access, budgets and opportunity to invent useful procedures. Include acquisition and maintenance costs, not only steady-state inference.

Where learning or reuse is claimed, distinguish acquisition tasks from subsequent evaluation and prevent answer leakage. Choose representative changed conditions and failures. State limits of the evaluator; independent agreement is not an oracle for open-ended research.

Give proposed completion criteria, an evidence plan, meaningful pivot or rejection conditions, and a dependency order. Separate component checks, integrated operation, qualification, independent verification and eventual acceptance. Do not invent universal numerical thresholds or present a future experiment as a result.

Sequence integration by ready capabilities. A producer first finishes and qualifies its agreed bounded release; the consumer then selects that exact artifact, checks compatibility and state migration, and qualifies its actual caller. Record unresolved releases as dependencies, not invented pins. Do not require every project or its entire future roadmap to finish before unrelated capabilities can integrate.

During independent development, design against clearly labeled contract candidates and consumer needs. Local architectural choices and implementation remain with each owner; unresolved cross-project assumptions stay explicit. The macro proposal may request or recommend a contract change, but cannot silently impose it. Include the compact contract documents needed to reduce context load and a route for exceptional deep inspection.

Preserve an actionable path toward hardware and biomimetic construction in parallel where useful. Defer expensive physical fidelity, fabrication and broad evolutionary campaigns until they retire a concrete uncertainty. Motor-based prototypes may be a pragmatic bridge, but must not silently replace the user's longer-term actuation ambition.
