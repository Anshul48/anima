## Objective and operating context

Find existing engineering and research practices that can be adapted into complete SOPs for how independent project architects and coordinators handle new user ideas.

The user supplies many ambitious ideas while projects are being built. They want minimal clerical work and retain control over direction, semantics and consequential choices. Agents own routine investigation, execution and reconciliation within their actual authority. Preserve ideas, distinguish exploration from instructions, keep authorized work moving, and support change without repeatedly restarting architecture.

Substrate, STC, SST, TRACE and TL have independent owners and changing implementations. Local design belongs with its project. Integration is planned through compact published contracts and later qualified through completed bounded releases and deliberate consumer pins.

Your output informs the macro architect, who must synthesize the findings and issue addressed SOP handoffs. Research does not itself establish architecture authority.

## Research questions

1. How should new input be captured, attributed, checked against prior decisions and routed without treating every suggestion as a requirement or every direct instruction as a future idea?
2. How should proposals and decisions preserve alternatives, reasons, supersession and deferred possibilities while keeping routine work lightweight?
3. How should consequential baseline changes be assessed, agreed and handed to execution without architecture churn or approval for every small step?
4. How should research questions become bounded evidence-producing investigations with claims, rival explanations, applicability limits and an honest stop decision?
5. How should independently owned projects negotiate contracts, finish bounded releases, qualify actual integration and revise procedures without importing mutable work or duplicating coordination?

Investigate these as a connected workflow. Avoid unrelated surveys of AGI, biology, robotics or every development methodology.

## Method

Start from original practice descriptions, official project processes, primary research and documented experience. Search beyond the seeds when it resolves a distinct mechanism or limitation. Verify actual contents before citing a source; distinguish recommendations from empirical evidence.

Compare a lightweight route and a more formal route where the tradeoff matters. Do not copy a large organization's committees, voting, waiting periods or tool stack into a small agent-led programme.

For each candidate practice record:

- The problem addressed and actual procedure.
- Primary source, author/organization, date/version or access frontier, and relevant section.
- What the source establishes, including empirical evidence or its absence.
- Applicability conditions and likely failure modes.
- Adaptation to this user's authority, attention budget and independent projects.
- What to reuse, change or reject, and why.

An official process is not proof of superior outcomes. Agent agreement is not experimental verification. A citation does not grant authority to impose a practice. Avoid unsupported productivity, token-saving or capability claims.

Respect source quotation limits; paraphrase and link. Preserve inaccessible-source gaps rather than reconstructing quotations from memory.

Use bounded specialists if available for distinct questions and one integrator. Otherwise work sequentially and perform a separate checking pass. Report the actual review arrangement. Stop once major choices have useful alternatives, evidence and failure cases and further searching repeats known distinctions. Identify the next discriminating observation for unresolved consequential questions.

## Complete procedure candidates

Provide usable candidates, not method names or empty templates. Each needs a trigger, responsible roles, inputs, steps, decision criteria, artifacts, exceptions, completion and authority limits.

Work through:

- A speculative mechanism arriving during an authorized build.
- A direct user instruction changing a requirement.
- A previously rejected idea with a materially new qualifier.
- An experiment yielding failure or an inconclusive result.
- A cross-project capability whose producer lacks a completed release.
- Background research arriving after an architectural choice was made.

Show what continues, what is recorded, what changes and what requires a consequential decision. Include the small everyday case. These are procedures supporting human/agent work, not implementations of the substrate's runtime plasticity.
