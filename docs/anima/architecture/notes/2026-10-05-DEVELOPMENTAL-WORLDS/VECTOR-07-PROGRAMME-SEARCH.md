# Vector 7 — Programme search across developing worlds

Architecture discussion, 2026-10-06. This is the full seventh-vector treatment in the current round. It develops the organizational search direction in [Expert worlds and search](EXPERT-WORLDS-AND-SEARCH.md), following [developmental change](VECTOR-01-DEVELOPMENTAL-CHANGE.md), [attention and commitments](VECTOR-02-ATTENTION-AND-COMMITMENTS.md), [expert individuality](VECTOR-03-EXPERT-INDIVIDUALITY.md), [physical construction](VECTOR-04-PHYSICAL-CONSTRUCTION.md), [creative coverage](VECTOR-05-CREATIVE-COVERAGE.md) and [construction repertoires](VECTOR-06-CONSTRUCTION-REPERTOIRES.md). Recommendations remain proposals while the user reads the round. No programme, search algorithm, runtime or organizational transformation is authorized by this essay.

**My current bet:** make a body of work navigable through revisable accounts of alternatives, conditions, resources, evidence and actual execution. Search should help identify useful continuations and, when warranted, develop a better formulation or organization of the work itself.

The user's 3A ambition is large: entire projects and an organization's workflows and branches could become a solution space that can be examined, extended and understood. A separate capability could construct that representation, with TRACE helping organize the accumulated material. The broader canvas includes research, construction, expert societies and physical development.

That ambition is worth preserving. Its decisive question is what the system can do with such a representation: discover a consequential missing condition, choose informative work, coordinate a useful construction, revise a failing decomposition, or retain a better way of working.

A navigable SST can expose those possibilities. The underlying programme also contains shared work, uncertain relationships and effects occurring in a world that continues to change.

## What a programme search is searching over

A programme is a sustained attempt to produce outcomes under goals, constraints and available means. Its work may include several projects, independent investigators, operational teams, tools and physical environments.

Some search choices concern a candidate artifact: which controller, interface, explanation or design to develop.

Others concern how to acquire evidence, which method to use, where to allocate scarce resources, how to divide responsibility, or whether the current problem formulation is adequate.

Those choices can affect one another. A new measurement method can make a previously speculative design assessable. A representation can expose a common subproblem. A changed physical design can invalidate the assumptions used by a control team.

The search object therefore includes candidate ways of organizing inquiry and construction, alongside candidate outputs.

Its boundary still matters. A programme can search within an accepted objective while investigating different interpretations of an unresolved requirement. A proposed change to the objective is a different kind of decision. Evidence about what works does not itself authorize that change.

The operating arrangement from 1A remains relevant: routine investigation and execution proceed under the authority already granted, while consequential direction belongs to its responsible owner. Programme search should reduce the amount of routine orchestration the user has to perform.

It should also keep costs visible. More alternatives can consume time, attention and assessment capacity without improving the eventual outcome.

## Several views of the same work

I would distinguish these concerns before selecting a graph format.

| View | What it contributes | An important distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Intent | Outcomes, requirements, governing constraints and unresolved meaning | A proposed interpretation versus an accepted objective |
| Alternatives | Candidate artifacts, methods, formulations, assignments and continuations | An available possibility versus an adopted action |
| Decomposition and dependencies | Joint requirements, prerequisites, compatibility and interactions | A useful decomposition versus a fact about what the world requires |
| Resources | Availability, capacity, reservations, consumption and release | A projected allocation versus resources actually available |
| Execution | Attempts, actual participants, partial effects and unfinished work | A submitted request versus an observed outcome |
| Evidence | Attributable observations, assessments and their conditions | A record of an event versus an interpretation of its significance |

These are semantic views. They need not become six services, six databases or six universally mandatory schemas.

A small programme may express most of them through existing tools and a few linked records. A developing society may need richer machinery because participants interpret conditions differently, share state selectively or change their own relationships.

One underlying artifact can participate in several views. A trial record can document execution, support an assessment and motivate a new alternative. Its significance should remain distinguishable from the record itself.

The views need enough common identity and binding to refer to the same work. They can retain different domain representations.

This is consistent with a world hosting other worlds: the host supports dependable relationships, while the meaning and consequences of domain work remain with the responsible participants.

## A tree can expose choices while the work remains a graph

Suppose a programme considers two designs. These are alternatives relative to a particular decision.

Each design also needs several contributions together: a model, suitable software, an applicable calibration and an assessment procedure. Those contributions form a conjunction within that candidate.

The two designs may share one contribution. They may also compete for a fixture or depend on incompatible versions of the same component.

A conventional branching picture can hide those differences. Duplicating a shared task beneath both branches can make it appear that two independent tasks or two independent results exist. Flattening a conjunction into several alternatives can make an incomplete construction look sufficient.

The representation needs to distinguish at least:

- Alternative ways to achieve a contribution.
- Contributions needed jointly by a particular construction.
- Shared artifacts or subproblems that several candidates may use.
- Conditions under which a dependency or interaction matters.

An alternative is rarely exclusive in every context. Two methods may compete for immediate adoption while both remain useful elsewhere. A decomposition can also be provisional: later evidence may show that the chosen subproblems cannot be solved independently.

A tree remains useful as a view centered on one question. It can unfold parts of a richer graph while preserving references to shared work. The display should not silently determine the semantics of the programme.

There are primary precedents for treating alternatives and joint subgoals explicitly. The [search-evidence note](VECTOR-07-SEARCH-EVIDENCE.md) examines formal and hierarchical planning systems, including their assumptions and assessment limits. Their modeled domains supply meanings that an organizational graph must often investigate.

Exhausting sampled moves or the current budget is different from establishing impossibility. HyperTree Proof Search makes the distinction concrete: a search node can be “invalid” after sampled tactics fail. Its subgoals and tactic validity come from a formal proving environment. [Lample et al., NeurIPS 2022 supplement](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/a8901c5e85fb8e1823bbf0f755053672-Supplemental-Conference.pdf)

Feedback also means that the entire programme need not be a DAG. A model informs an experiment; an experiment changes the model; a later iteration changes the construction.

A bounded execution segment can expose ordering constraints without forcing that developmental loop into an acyclic global ontology. Iteration identity and actual events help distinguish a legitimate feedback process from a contradictory prerequisite cycle.

## Constructing the view is substantive work

The user's separate graph-building capability has an important responsibility.

Organizational records usually contain mixtures of plans, decisions, informal language, artifacts, status reports and observations. They rarely constitute an adequate search model without interpretation.

The builder might reconcile references, propose candidate decompositions, identify shared dependencies, expose disagreements and produce a view relevant to one decision.

Its output remains an attributable interpretation of accessible material. A persuasive diagram can still omit the condition that matters most.

Consider the sentence “the controller depends on the sensor model.” It could mean that a particular controller requires that model at runtime, that its design used the model, that an evaluation assumed it, or that an investigator thinks the model may explain its failure.

Those meanings lead to different continuations. Treating all of them as a hard prerequisite could block useful work; treating all as loose association could permit an invalid construction.

A useful builder preserves uncertainty and the basis for a relationship. It should also distinguish a missing relation from an established absence.

The relevant scope is the decision at hand. The system need not reconstruct an entire organization perfectly before a local search becomes useful.

It can begin with an incomplete view, inspect the dependencies that a consequential choice relies on, and refine the view as work produces evidence.

Representation acquisition, reconciliation and maintenance belong in the cost account. If maintaining the map consumes the programme's capacity, its apparent comprehensiveness has become a liability.

This proposal does not assume access to a private laboratory's or company's data. The user's organizational example supplies ambition; actual evidence access retains its own boundaries.

## A dependency needs meaning and conditions

A dependency can express necessity, ordering, compatibility, helpfulness or interference. These are different relationships.

A calibration may be required before interpreting a measurement. An exploratory trial may improve a model without being necessary for every candidate. An optimization may degrade a property valued by another team.

A shared artifact also needs an applicable binding. The same calibration file may support two candidates only if its conditions cover both. Sharing the file's identity does not establish that.

The relationship's status matters. A dependency can be inferred, reported, experimentally supported or required by a governing contract. Revising an inferred edge and changing a governing constraint involve different responsibilities.

The primary TÆMS framework provides a useful historical precedent: it models computational task interrelationships and separates the task environment from agents' partial views, including when information is available and what obtaining it costs. Its stated scope concerns computational coordination, rather than a complete model of physical organizations. [Decker and Lesser, AAAI 1993](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1993/AAAI93-033.pdf)

The architectural inference is to represent interactions that can change a consequential choice. ANIMA does not need to adopt that framework or make every effect quantitatively known.

A proposed relationship can carry a conditional expectation while a precisely modeled relationship supports a narrower dependable claim. Neither should acquire stronger status through placement in the graph.

Completing all listed tasks also does not prove that the list captured the intended outcome. Decomposition is itself a hypothesis that can be assessed and revised.

SHOP2's planning correctness is conditional on a correct supplied domain description. That boundary matters here: a rigorous search over an incomplete organizational model can still miss the condition determining the real outcome. [Nau et al., JAIR 2003](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~nau/papers/nau2003shop2.pdf)

## Evidence has to remain connected to the work it describes

A result matters under the conditions that produced it: candidate versions, inputs, procedures, participants, operating regimes and assessment methods.

Search can reuse an observation across several branches when its meaning and applicability justify that reuse. The same observation does not become several independent confirmations because it appears under several candidates.

Likewise, results from different teams can share a blind spot through a common simulator, dataset, evaluator or interpretation. Agreement may be informative while remaining correlated.

The programme should distinguish improving a candidate from improving the evidence used to compare candidates. A broad search can exploit a weak assessment mechanism and produce increasingly attractive scores.

Sometimes the appropriate next work is a better measurement, a contrasting case or a review of the objective's interpretation.

New evidence can also change which earlier results remain applicable. A changed sensor model might affect some evaluations while leaving unrelated work intact.

That suggests bounded revision around the relevant assumptions and consumers. It does not require indiscriminately invalidating the whole programme whenever a version changes.

A score should have a meaning that the responsible world can explain. Scores from different worlds may concern different outcomes and cannot automatically be added or compared.

The programme owner relates local consequences to its objectives. The domain assessor establishes what its evidence supports. A globally convenient ranking should not erase that distinction.

## Search possibilities and actual execution are different state

A branch describes work that could be attempted. Execution records what participants actually do.

A request can be accepted without starting. Work can start and produce partial artifacts. An execution can finish while its result fails the intended assessment.

Pending work has its own state. It supplies neither a failure result nor evidence of success.

This distinction is especially important when search runs asynchronously. A new result can make a pending activity less useful, but cancellation may leave consumed resources, partial effects or unfinished responsibilities.

Those consequences retain an owner. Dropping a branch from an active search view does not cancel its actual work or discharge an obligation.

A local world can manage a compound procedure internally. The programme need not observe every microstep. It needs an adequate account of exported effects, limits and continuation responsibilities.

A search revision can therefore propose a new continuation without immediately enacting it. Execution uses the applicable local authority and current conditions.

Standing delegation should cover ordinary work. This is a semantic boundary between search and effects, rather than an invitation to ask the human for approval at every branch.

## Parallel search shares real resources

Several branches can be investigated in parallel while sharing one fixture, one specialist, one dataset or one integration environment.

A copied search state does not create another physical resource. An assumed reservation is also different from an actual allocation.

The relevant resource constraints can concern capacity, timing, access, compatibility or effects on the resource itself. A trial may occupy a fixture; it may also change its calibration or consume a specimen.

Compute is only one dimension. Human attention, engineering effort, instrument access, elapsed time and recovery work can constrain progress.

The user explicitly wants to model such bottlenecks. A useful programme view should expose the limiting resource at the level where a decision can affect it.

Adding workers can accelerate independent construction while leaving a measurement or integration bottleneck unchanged. It can also increase the number of results waiting for assessment.

Shared work can produce the opposite benefit: one appropriate investigation may inform several candidates. Its cost should be charged once to the actual work, with applicability and correlated-error limits preserved.

A coordination method should account for the opportunity cost of occupying a shared resource. An activity can have positive local value while delaying a more consequential programme contribution.

These relationships can remain uncertain. The system may estimate duration and contention, revise those estimates through execution, and retain the difference between a forecast and an observed schedule.

## Physical branches cannot all be realized from the same past

Software and simulation make some candidate states easy to copy. Their copies still have costs and shared external dependencies.

Physical intervention has a stronger limitation. A trial can change the specimen, apparatus or surrounding conditions. An earlier state may no longer be available for a direct comparison.

A branch evaluated after another branch's intervention may therefore be answering a different question from the one shown in the original tree.

The programme needs an account of actual intervention order and relevant conditions. A physical assessor determines whether a later observation supports the intended comparison.

Simulation can help explore counterfactuals under its model. It does not restore the earlier physical state or establish that the model captures the disputed mechanism.

Cancellation and recovery also have domain meanings. Closing a process, compensating a database operation and stopping a physical intervention are different capabilities.

The substrate can support invocation identity, lifecycle and continuing custody. The local world supplies the operational semantics.

This is a substantive reason to keep programme alternatives, actual execution and evidence connected but distinguishable.

## A hypothetical programme for a capable laboratory hand

Suppose a programme seeks a hand that can transfer a range of fragile containers under specified operating conditions. Its objectives include useful handling, damage avoidance and practical turnaround. The requirements are assumed to be declared for this example; no hardware design or acceptance threshold is selected here.

A mechanical team develops contact geometry and compliance. A control team develops estimation and action policies. A measurement team operates the test fixture and interprets trial results.

Three initial approaches are plausible:

1. Improve control using the existing mechanical arrangement.
2. Change contact geometry or compliance, with a suitable control adaptation.
3. Develop sensing, mechanics and control together.

These are alternatives relative to the programme's next substantial investment. Several activities can still proceed across them while the decision remains open.

Each candidate needs joint contributions. The mechanical design must be realizable, its controller must fit its behavior, and the assessment must measure the properties the programme values.

There is shared work: some fixture characterization, source material and observation-processing machinery may serve several candidates. There is also conditional reuse. A calibration valid for one arrangement may fail to cover another.

The test fixture has limited availability. Simulation, code construction and retrospective analysis can continue while physical trials await it. Those activities need not wait for one globally selected branch.

The first programme view treats the main uncertainty as controller quality. A control candidate improves simulated performance, while a mechanical candidate takes longer to become testable.

A superficial ranking favors the control branch. The programme should ask what that ranking measures and which assumptions support it.

Suppose a physical trial then shows delayed contact observations. Two explanations remain live: the sensor interpretation may be inadequate, or the mechanical behavior may differ from the model. The observed failure does not settle either explanation.

The result affects several branches because they relied on a related observation model. It does not prove that every candidate using the same sensor is invalid.

The measurement and control worlds propose an investigation that can distinguish observation timing from the response being measured. The first step may improve no handling score by itself; it enables a later model comparison.

Meanwhile, the mechanical team can continue work that does not depend on the disputed assumption. Its available construction actions are different from the control team's available diagnostic actions.

The fixture's actual state matters. An earlier trial changed the contact arrangement, so a new measurement is associated with that arrangement and its relevant conditions. The graph builder should not attach it indiscriminately to every historical configuration.

Suppose further assessment supports a useful distinction among commanded action, observed contact and inferred load. The original handoff between teams did not carry that distinction adequately.

The programme now has several revisions to consider: improve the observation representation, revise controller assumptions, adapt the mechanical model, or maintain a joint estimation-and-control method.

These are changes to the search formulation and available construction machinery. Choosing a different candidate within the old formulation would not necessarily resolve the obstacle.

A temporary coalition can develop the joint method. The measurement world retains calibration interpretation, the control world retains its policy semantics, and the mechanical world retains embodiment and realization knowledge.

They may acquire shared intermediate state and an exported capability for investigating a particular class of contact failures. If repeated use justifies it, that coalition can become a maintained participant.

Its proposed benefit is assessed through actual handling under the relevant conditions. A successful investigation, an improved model and a completed hand remain different achievements.

A later container family can test whether the acquired method transfers. Changed conditions may require adaptation, or make the old method unsuitable.

The programme has consequently gained several possible outputs: a candidate hand, better evidence, a useful state distinction, a diagnostic procedure and a more productive relationship among worlds.

Those gains can justify different retention decisions. They need not all remain active in the programme's working graph.

The consequential decision in this example is whether to continue investing within the initial controller-centered formulation or devote work to a coupled observation, embodiment and control formulation. Its answer depends on evidence, feasible continuations, costs and the accepted objectives.

Nothing in this hypothetical establishes that coupling is always best. A simpler control repair may be sufficient; a measurement correction may remove the apparent need for mechanical change. Search should preserve those alternatives until the evidence warrants a narrower conclusion.

## Choosing what work is worth doing next

Programme search can allocate effort among improving candidates, strengthening evidence, investigating dependencies, changing a formulation and developing reusable capability.

Uncertainty alone is an inadequate ranking. Resolving a large uncertainty may leave every consequential choice unchanged. A smaller uncertainty may determine whether an expensive construction is usable.

The relevant question is what the work could change: the immediate decision, a feasible continuation, the quality of assessment or the system's later ability to construct and investigate.

Different horizons matter. An operation that creates a reusable measurement method can have value beyond the current programme. An urgent repair can be valuable even if it produces little transferable knowledge.

Those contributions require explicit reasoning about scope and cost. Calling a task “research” does not give it unlimited prospective value.

The [allocation-evidence note](VECTOR-07-ALLOCATION-EVIDENCE.md) examines computation selection, staged evaluation and resource-aware scheduling. These precedents address different parts of the problem under specified models; they do not provide an omniscient programme allocator.

The inspected computation-selection model makes competing alternatives relevant to computation priorities and shows how a one-step policy can stop prematurely. Its modeled evidence and costs delimit that result. [Hay et al., v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1207.5879v1)

Some work has joint value. An intermediate observation may enable a second investigation whose result changes the decision. A policy considering only immediate improvement can prematurely abandon that sequence.

Conversely, repeatedly promising a later payoff can sustain an unproductive branch. The programme needs bounded opportunities to pursue such sequences and observations that can revise their justification.

The architecture can accommodate cheap rules, domain heuristics, learned allocation policies and deliberate long-horizon choices. Their usefulness should be assessed against what the work actually produces.

A scalar score can help within a scoped comparison. Across distinct objectives, resources and consequences, the owner may need several measures and explicit tradeoffs.

## Screening should preserve the meaning of an unfinished candidate

Cheap preliminary assessment can save work when it is informative about the intended outcome. Its omissions and biases need to be understood well enough for the allocation decision.

Hyperband illustrates the issue in a specific setting: low-resource performance can be uninformative about later performance, and evaluation overhead affects realized benefit. [Li et al., published JMLR article](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume18/16-558/16-558.pdf)

A research candidate may develop slowly because a prerequisite, representation or assessment method is missing. Absent evaluation should remain distinct from observed poor quality.

Stopping further allocation can be reasonable without establishing that a method cannot work. Changed evidence or a repaired dependency may justify reopening it, provided the necessary artifacts and conditions remain available.

Conversely, a dormant branch should not acquire an unlimited claim on future resources through speculative promise. Reconsideration needs a reason connected to the current programme or a justified developmental opportunity.

The search record should preserve the cause of stopping: cost, incompatibility, a failed assessment, inadequate evidence, or a limited search attempt. These causes support different future interpretations.

## Bound the cost of deciding how to work

Exact reasoning over every possible future investigation, construction and organizational change would itself consume the programme.

A useful approximation focuses on live decisions and feasible work that can affect them. It can reopen the formulation when evidence indicates that the current alternatives are inadequate.

The programme can keep several activities active with different horizons. A focused construction path can coexist with a bounded investigation of a representation gap or a reusable method.

Asynchronous foraging supports that arrangement. Results should return with the question they address, the conditions inspected and what would change if the finding holds.

Background work is not costless or automatically independent. It can compete for models, instruments, assessment and attention; its findings can also depend on the same uncertain assumptions as the main path.

A result that arrives late is assessed against the current decision and conditions. It may remain useful as a lesson or as evidence for another programme even when its original branch has closed.

The user should receive consequential choices with alternatives, implications and a recommendation grounded in the available evidence. Routine bookkeeping, source reconciliation and technical investigation remain work for the authorized participants.

This preserves their role in direction and synthesis while allowing a larger society of agents, people, tools and physical systems to do progressively more realization work.

The performance question is verified progress and useful capability per relevant resource, including human attention. Maintaining an elaborate account of progress is not itself that progress.

## Nested worlds expose conditional contributions

A parent programme needs enough information about a child world to use its contribution. That need does not imply importing the child's whole internal state or forcing it into the parent's ontology.

A compact result can expose its meaning, supported conditions, evidence status, relevant dependencies and continuing obligations.

The parent can ask for deeper investigation when a consequential decision turns on an omitted condition. Access and interpretation remain governed by the applicable relationships.

One world may serve several parents. Its resource commitments and exported conditions therefore matter beyond a single containment tree.

Likewise, a composite can depend on contributions from worlds with different representations. A common envelope can identify the work while translations and domain meanings remain revisable.

The responsible participants need to understand what an exchange changes. An advisory finding, a resource commitment, a capability invocation and a transfer of state custody have different effects.

A useful programme view makes those distinctions available at the level where they affect action. It need not expose every internal detail at every level.

The following sketch shows the intended relationship among views, search and work. It is a conceptual loop, rather than a selected implementation or an automatic promotion path.

~~~mermaid
flowchart LR
    S["Attributable source and project records"] --> V["Decision view: alternatives, conditions, resources"]
    V --> Q["SST explores useful continuations"]
    Q --> P["Proposed work or revision"]
    P --> W["Responsible world executes within its authority"]
    W --> E["Observed effects and artifacts"]
    E --> A["Domain assessment"]
    E --> V
    A --> V
    E --> T["TRACE history and lessons within granted scope"]
    T --> V
~~~

The arrow from proposal to world means that the world handles the request under its actual authority and conditions. The arrow from assessment to view means that supported findings can revise an interpretation. Neither arrow establishes universal correctness.

## Programme organization can itself develop

The broader substrate ambition adds something beyond selecting a schedule for fixed teams.

Repeated work can show that two participants need shared intermediate state, a different communication method or a joint investigation capability.

A decomposition can change because an assumed independent subproblem depends on conditions maintained elsewhere. A useful diagnostic can become part of the construction repertoire from Vector 6.

A temporary coalition may become a persistent composite. A composite may later specialize or separate when its costs or demands change.

These transitions alter actual machinery and responsibility. A new label on the programme diagram is insufficient evidence that the integration occurred.

The programme should be able to propose and assess such changes. The substrate should accommodate them while keeping affected state, ongoing work, conditions and authority intelligible.

For example, the laboratory-hand coalition might initially exchange completed reports. Its later joint method might continuously maintain an estimate and choose the next informative action.

That transition changes the participants' cooperation, the state they share and the capability they export. It can improve later work if the new arrangement addresses the observed bottleneck.

It may also introduce synchronization cost, common errors or excessive coupling. The original independent arrangement deserves comparison.

Organization is therefore one adaptation surface among several. The system can change representations, methods, resources or relationships as the evidence warrants.

The frontier hypothesis is that repeated programme work develops useful construction organization and retains advantages across unfamiliar demands. Searching more assignments among fixed participants is one possible contribution, but it does not establish that broader development.

Evolutionary populations and long-term inheritance can be investigated later. Useful local organization can already develop through interaction, guided search and human architecture.

## The strong simpler alternative deserves equal freedom

A capable coordinator can already use project tools, dependency graphs, versioned artifacts, searchable decisions, resource-aware scheduling, specialist consultation and domain evaluators.

It can revise a decomposition, invent a procedure and investigate a contradiction. It should receive the same evidence, tools, models, authority and learning opportunities as the proposed environment.

A comparison against a static checklist would mostly measure the advantage of giving one system more useful machinery.

The proposed graph-builder and SST arrangement should earn its place through better decisions, useful realized work, less consequential rework or retained capability under a complete account of costs.

Possible gains include making a shared dependency easier to notice, reusing an investigation across candidates, or carrying a conditional lesson into a later programme.

Possible losses include expensive map maintenance, misleading abstractions, unnecessary consultations and delayed execution while the system elaborates speculative structure.

Many programmes may need only a modest decision view and ordinary coordination. A richer environment becomes worthwhile where its retained distinctions and operations produce a meaningful benefit.

The ambition can remain large while particular mechanisms earn their adoption locally.

## A comparison that can reveal the mechanism

I would structure a later comparison around one consequential decision within a bounded programme, then examine what transfers to another programme.

This essay does not launch that comparison or set acceptance thresholds.

The initial tasks should have an interpretable intended outcome, usable domain assessment and some shared or interacting work. They should permit several continuations without making every alternative equally plausible.

A controlled fixture can establish mechanics such as conjunctive requirements, stale bindings or resource contention. A realistic programme is needed to assess whether those mechanisms improve consequential work outside the planted cases.

Several comparisons can separate possible explanations for a gain.

**Representation:** hold candidate methods and search opportunities comparable while varying whether shared work, conditional dependencies and evidence are adequately represented. This asks what the view contributes.

**Search:** give alternatives access to comparable observations and total resources while comparing ordinary coordination with bounded exploration over the view. This asks what the search contributes.

**Execution:** examine whether recommended continuations remain useful when work encounters shared resources, delays, partial effects and changes in actual conditions. A good recommendation and a successfully realized programme are different endpoints.

**Development:** allow comparable opportunities to investigate, invent methods and revise organization. Examine whether explicit retained changes improve later work beyond what ordinary tools and text memory preserve.

**Assessment:** include plausible local improvements that fail to produce the intended combined behavior. This tests whether the programme notices an inadequate decomposition or evaluator rather than merely selecting the largest local score.

These distinctions need not require a large factorial campaign before the first useful result. A focused comparison can target the uncertainty that would change a near-term design choice.

The baseline should be able to make improvements too. Giving only the proposed system memory, specialists or permission to create tools would confound the interpretation.

Report actual outcomes and incomplete work, alongside acquisition, observation, search, evaluation, execution, repair, reconciliation, maintenance and attention costs. Graph construction and unsuccessful investigation count.

Shared work should not be counted several times merely because several branches use it. Nor should shared evidence be treated as independent replication.

Delayed results and closed branches retain their observed status. A task omitted from a later report must not silently become a successful or irrelevant task.

Diagnostic assistance belongs in a separate account. Supplying the decisive missing dependency can show why a choice failed; it does not establish autonomous discovery of that dependency.

Later demands should examine unfamiliar conditions, changed dependencies and opportunities to reuse or adapt the acquired method. A retrospectively attractive map of one finished programme is a different achievement.

No bounded benchmark can establish competence over an entire research organization. It can identify mechanisms worth extending and conditions under which they fail.

## The map should stay useful as it becomes larger

The scale target is access to the right structure at the right resolution.

A high-level view can expose major alternatives, bottlenecks and unsettled questions. A lower-level view can investigate the conditions needed for a particular action.

Some work belongs to several views, and some relevant information remains outside the current organization. The discovery concerns from Vector 5 continue to apply.

A programme map needs revision and selective activation. Historical branch bodies need not occupy the working view indefinitely.

Retirement from that view can reduce selection and maintenance costs while consequential history remains available through the responsible retention mechanisms. Availability should be checked where a later decision depends on it.

This does not assume that TL supplies a complete immutable history after reclamation, or that every retired branch leaves a permanent active tombstone.

TRACE can help retain interpretations and conditional lessons within its granted duties. Source custody and historical preservation retain their distinct contracts.

A smaller active view can be useful if it preserves the routes and commitments required by current work. A large graph is neither a complete organizational model nor evidence of stronger reasoning.

## What I would carry into the next design stage

I propose five principles for later consideration.

1. **Distinguish meanings within the work.** Alternatives, joint contributions, dependencies, resources, execution and evidence can share identity without sharing status.
2. **Treat the decision view as revisable.** Its relationships are accountable interpretations of available material, with uncertainty and routes to investigate consequential gaps.
3. **Connect search to actual consequences.** Proposed continuations, enacted work, partial effects and assessed outcomes remain distinguishable and attributable.
4. **Permit development of the work itself.** Representations, methods, division of responsibility and participant relationships can change through experience.
5. **Judge programme value against capable alternatives.** Count complete costs and later usefulness, including the possibility that ordinary coordination is sufficient.

The [ANIMA responsibility map](../../RESPONSIBILITIES.md) supplies the current ownership boundary. SST offers bounded exploration without owning the domain verdict. Responsible worlds and programme owners retain execution and outcome interpretation. STC is the construction-world implementation for software cases; physical and research worlds need their own realization semantics. TRACE and TL contribute their distinct memory and source responsibilities. The broad substrate supports hosting, cooperation, resources and controlled organizational change.

The first design commitment I would seek later is a dependable, revisable relationship between candidate programme structure, attributable evidence and actual execution. A particular graph language, global scheduler, search policy or runtime topology can remain open.

The useful front door is one consequential question whose alternatives and shared conditions can be made navigable. The long-term ambition is a society that learns better ways to investigate, construct and organize across such questions.

Vector 8 can then examine what a successor inherits, what should remain accessible through history, and what should be retired or reacquired.
