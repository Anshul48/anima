# Creativity, selective context, and durable constraints

Date: 2026-10-05. Status: proposed architecture analysis; no recommendation here is ratified. Scope: supplied 4B and 4C material, with bounded primary-source research. ANIMA is the notebook. No earlier project or conversation was opened, and no implementation is specified.

The useful synthesis is a system that can construct unfamiliar possibilities while remembering what it has agreed to do. The user’s intent combines creative dynamics with two control functions: select relevant information for each consequential micro-step, and preserve commitments and consistency across long histories. Expert worlds, predictions at several compression levels, and errors travelling upward provide a possible setting for these functions. Knowledge outside the active graph remains recoverable; limited attention need not imply literal biological forgetting. The weaker-model anatomical account is a hypothesis to inspect, not evidence or authority.

Creativity and control can coexist throughout reasoning. A candidate may depart from learned expectations while still respecting an explicit task commitment. Conversely, a familiar candidate may violate a commitment. One novelty-versus-control score would conflate these cases. The proposed architecture therefore distinguishes what deserves attention, what is expected, what has been accepted, and what can actually be checked.

The examined neuroscience supports several ingredients, but not an exclusive anatomical birthplace or a universal cascade. Nakazawa et al. disrupted CA3 NMDA-receptor function in mice and found impaired associative spatial recall under reduced cues, with disrupted plasticity at recurrent synapses. This supports a contribution to pattern completion: recovering an existing representation from incomplete evidence. It does not establish that every new idea originates in CA3 or that random recombination is its unique mechanism. Pattern completion can also reinforce familiar answers; novelty requires something beyond successful retrieval. [Nakazawa et al., Science 2002](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1071795)

A May 2026 human intracranial study is particularly relevant. In 28 epilepsy patients performing two LEGO-like inference tasks, He et al. report ripple-associated replay that reorganizes building blocks into candidate sequences and updates mPFC representations toward inferred compositional solutions. This supports temporally coordinated recombination and cortical updating in structured inference. It does not demonstrate that a ripple always contains a complete new idea, originates in a particular hippocampal subfield, or causes all later creativity. The accessible material was the publisher abstract, figure titles and references; the full article was subscription-only, so causal and methodological claims beyond that material remain unverified here. [He et al., Nature Neuroscience 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02291-3)

The specific schema-loosening claim has a real primary preprint behind it. He et al.’s July 2025 author abstract reports increased ripples during creative generation, reduced ripple-triggered mPFC high-frequency activity, and patterns diverging from typical schema-like task states, strongest in vmPFC. These are preliminary author-reported observations supporting their interpretation of transient schema disengagement. They do not establish a necessary causal switch for creativity. The 2026 publication still cites this work as a preprint. Direct bioRxiv HTML and PDF returned 403; its full methods, sample and controls were not independently inspected. [He et al., bioRxiv 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.07.22.666046)

Beaty et al. found default and executive network coupling during ongoing creative idea production, with stronger early default–salience coupling and later default–executive coupling in their task. This supports changing cooperation rather than an executive system that begins only after an entire bottom-up sequence has finished. These fMRI connectivity findings cannot establish a universal causal order. Also, mPFC is among default-network regions in their analysis; treating mPFC updating and DMN participation as entirely separate consecutive modules obscures overlap. [Beaty et al., Scientific Reports 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep10964)

For 4C, Chatham, Frank and Badre tested input and output gating demands in a hierarchical working-memory task. Their reported frontostriatal findings support the useful distinction between admitting information to working memory and selecting which already-held information influences behaviour. They do not establish a biological mechanism for indefinite commitment enforcement. The amygdala reference in the supplied summary remains an analogy here: motivational significance, uncertainty and novelty should be separate candidate signals, not collapsed into one named brain region or equated with authorization. [Chatham et al., Neuron 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3955887/)

Four distinctions would govern the proposed computational interpretation:

| Category | Meaning | Proposed effect | What it cannot authorize |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Learned salience | Estimated relevance, surprise, urgency or information value for the present decision | Allocate attention and retrieval effort | Creating or cancelling obligations |
| Schema preference | A learned expectation about plausible, useful or familiar structure | Guide generation; soften during bounded exploration | Treating unfamiliarity as invalidity |
| Task commitment | A sourced, scoped obligation accepted within the task | Condition relevant actions until fulfilled, revised or superseded | Treating an inferred preference or model promise as user ratification |
| Checkable invariant | A precise condition evaluated over specified representations or actions | Accept or reject within the checker’s declared coverage | Certifying unrepresented meaning or unseen consequences |

These categories are functional distinctions, not a proposed brain map. An obligation can be clear while its applicability to a novel situation is uncertain. A preference can be strong without being binding. A prior factual claim can deserve rechecking without becoming a commitment to repeat it forever.

The following contracts describe observable responsibilities rather than implementation interfaces. Under a proposed division of labour, TRACE would preserve recoverable evidence and relationships, while SST would coordinate bounded construction, prediction and evaluation. That ownership is itself a proposal.

| Proposed function | Required behaviour and evidence | Limit of its decision rights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context selection | Assemble the current objective, applicable commitments, relevant evidence, competing interpretations and consequential unknowns. Keep source references and permit additional retrieval when a prediction fails. | Ranking something low does not prove it irrelevant; selection cannot erase durable history. |
| Commitment continuity | Recover the accepted obligation, its source, scope, status and authorized changes. Distinguish completion, revision, contradiction and unresolved applicability. | New model-generated prose cannot silently supersede an accepted commitment. |
| Association and construction | Combine episodes, concepts and relationships into alternative candidates; mark retrieved facts and inferred connections separately. | A coherent candidate is not established knowledge. |
| Replay and prediction | Explore short candidate trajectories within expert worlds and across abstraction levels; expose dependencies, expected consequences and disagreement. | Internally consistent simulation is not external validation. |
| Constraint evaluation | Apply explicit checks where coverage is known, and make semantic judgments with supporting evidence and uncertainty where necessary. | An LLM classification is not a proof; a checker only covers the condition it evaluates. |
| Novelty preservation | Retain unusual, deferred and failed candidates with provenance and failure reasons, while restricting the active set. | Retention does not imply truth, usefulness or promotion. |

A candidate loop starts by selecting context for a consequential decision, then retrieving familiar and potentially contrasting relationships. Construction generates several possibilities. Short replay predicts their consequences; unexpected results request missing context, revise an interpretation or challenge a learned schema. Evaluation can occur during construction and after elaboration. A bounded exploratory pass may reduce schema penalties without weakening applicable commitments. Promising possibilities receive further discriminating checks, and rejected candidates remain recoverable. This is a proposed search dynamic, not a reenactment of hippocampal physiology.

“Retrieve only what is needed” should be an objective evaluated against downstream success, not a promise of perfect foresight. The selector cannot always know which information matters before reasoning with it. A small allowance for contrasting evidence and expansion on unresolved dependencies may prevent premature narrowing. Likewise, predictions at several levels should remain connected to inspectable evidence; repeatedly summarizing summaries can conceal the exception that reverses a conclusion.

The feasibility claim is bounded. External memory, selective retrieval, explicit commitments, candidate histories and concrete checks are computationally realizable mechanisms. Their combination does not guarantee semantic relevance or reliable long-horizon behaviour. Liu et al. demonstrated position-sensitive retrieval and question-answering performance in the models they tested, including long-context models. This motivates evaluating how evidence is used rather than assuming that stored or supplied context is effective context. It is historical evidence, not a measurement of the current intended model. [Liu et al., TACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/)

MultiChallenge separates instruction retention, implicit-context inference, version editing and self-coherence. Its original evaluation found substantial failures in historical models and improved judge agreement using focused, human-written instance rubrics rather than raw-history judging. This supports decomposing 4C into testable functions, while showing that semantic grading itself can fail. The benchmark deliberately selected challenging cases; its scores are neither present-day model rankings nor ordinary task failure rates. Its rubric results also do not establish that an LLM can reliably create those rubrics itself. [MultiChallenge, ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.958/)

Formal enforcement reaches only represented conditions: a resource limit, permission boundary or declared state relationship may admit a precise check. “Remain consistent with the research direction” usually requires interpretation. Translating that sentence into a formal condition introduces assumptions; a passing check may certify the translation rather than the intended meaning. Semantic evaluation should therefore distinguish supported, conflicting and unresolved cases, with narrower claims when coverage is partial. Multiple model judges may share errors. Agreement does not make an unresolved interpretation authoritative.

Consistency also needs revision rights. Automatically preserving every prior statement would entrench mistakes, outdated facts and abandoned plans. Commitments should follow explicit authority and scope; beliefs should remain revisable through evidence. When two obligations conflict, the architecture should expose the conflict and any applicable precedence, rather than invent an exception or silently reinterpret either obligation. Routine uncertainty can prompt narrower retrieval and checking; unresolved consequential authority or task meaning belongs in the user’s decision space.

The strongest counterargument is that this could become expensive machinery around ordinary retrieval and generation. Every extra gate introduces classification errors and context overhead. A simpler baseline may achieve equal or better performance. Another objection is that expert worlds and replay can amplify a shared misconception: several internally consistent simulations may all omit the real constraint. Their disagreement is informative, but their agreement needs independent evidence. A third objection is that protecting novelty can accumulate low-quality candidates indefinitely. Recoverable archival retention must not imply unlimited active evaluation. Finally, a commitment record can preserve the wrong interpretation with excellent fidelity. These objections make measurable incremental benefit, faithful source access and explicit uncertainty more important than anatomical resemblance.

Three questions could discriminate useful mechanisms without committing to a full programme:

1. Under matched retrieval and model-token budgets, does recombination plus short replay and protected novelty improve valid novel solutions on held-out compositional problems with misleading familiar schemas, compared with retrieval-only and ordinary generate-and-score loops? Separate validity, novelty and usefulness.
2. Does decision-conditioned context selection with recovery on prediction error retain more relevant exceptions and applicable commitments than static similarity retrieval? Include implicit dependencies, stale versions and plausible distractors; measure omissions and recovery cost as well as accuracy.
3. Across long histories containing authorized revisions and ambiguous semantic obligations, do sourced commitment records plus limited explicit checks reduce violations without freezing incorrect beliefs or blocking legitimate novelty? Compare formal coverage, semantic judgment error and false blocking separately.

The open architecture decision is how much these functions need explicit separation, learned coordination or both. The supplied intent supports investigating creative construction and durable control together. It does not yet ratify a module roster, anatomical sequence, enforcement guarantee or promotion rule.
