# Vector 8 evidence note: retaining competence and enabling descendants

Date of inspection: 2026-10-07. Status: bounded documentary evidence plus independent architectural proposals; no proposal is a ratified requirement. Companion essay: [Vector 8 — Retention and descendants](VECTOR-08-RETENTION-AND-DESCENDANTS.md).

The architectural ambition is cumulative development: a successor should inherit useful conditional competence and remain capable of revising assumptions. It should not require a lifetime reset to become adaptable. Three primary studies were selected because they protect different things: parameter configurations, performance on retained examples, and previously learned output behaviour. These mechanisms help separate retaining an ability from storing the experiences that produced it. None establishes unlimited developmental improvement, exact historical reconstruction, or a biological circuit that ANIMA must reproduce.

This note continues the distinction between access and useful remembering in [Vector 5's evidence note](VECTOR-05-NEUROSCIENCE-EVIDENCE.md), and between compact repertoire and prospective capability in [Vector 6's evidence note](VECTOR-06-LIBRARY-EVIDENCE.md). Those earlier studies are not re-summarized here.

## Three inspected primary studies

### 1. Elastic Weight Consolidation: protecting parameter behaviour

Kirkpatrick et al., *Overcoming catastrophic forgetting in neural networks*: [fixed arXiv:1612.00796v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.00796v2), 2017-01-25. Inspected §§2–3, Appendix 4.1–4.2 and associated figures/tables in the 13-page preprint; the PNAS final was not inspected. EWC adds a diagonal-Fisher-weighted quadratic penalty around learned parameters. Permuted-MNIST classification and ten-game Atari sequences demonstrate reduced forgetting. Atari games can recur; the system also uses inferred task contexts, task-specific gains/biases and replay buffers. Protection starts after 20 million game frames; Fisher estimation samples 100 minibatches. Performance remains below separate game networks. MNIST hyperparameter search tries 50 combinations where used. Atari buffer capacities differ between prose and table, so an exact storage budget cannot be inferred confidently. The inspected text provides no complete lifetime cost account. Approximate importance and selected task performance do not guarantee universal preservation, complete episodes, or justified belief revision.

### 2. Gradient Episodic Memory: protecting sampled experience

Lopez-Paz and Ranzato, *Gradient Episodic Memory for Continual Learning*: [published NIPS 2017 PDF](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2017/file/f87522788a2be2d171666752f97ddebb-Paper.pdf). Inspected §§2–4.4 and 6, including Tables 1–3, in the 10-page main paper; the linked supplemental ZIP could not be fetched. GEM keeps examples per task and projects updates against old-task memory gradients. Non-interference depends on local linearity/small steps and representative memories. Experiments use 20-task streams of permuted/rotated MNIST and incremental CIFAR-100, principally one pass over incoming data while memories are revisited. CIFAR uses task-specific classifiers. Evaluation measures test accuracy and forward/backward transfer; beneficial backward transfer occurs. Larger memories improve sampled CIFAR results. Reported MNIST CPU training times are 77/135 seconds versus 11/11 for the single predictor, excluding any demonstrated complete lifecycle account. Each iteration needs old-task backward passes. Neither sampled loss protection nor aggregate accuracy guarantees every episode or future condition is retained.

### 3. Learning without Forgetting: protecting inherited responses

Li and Hoiem, *Learning without Forgetting*: [fixed arXiv:1606.09282v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.09282v1), 2016-06-29, an ECCV submission preprint. Inspected §§3–5, tables and associated figures in the 16-page version, not later revisions or the published final. LwF records old-task probabilities on new-task images, adds output parameters, and trains shared/old/new parameters with supervised and distillation losses. Public pretrained ImageNet/Places2 networks acquire VOC, bird or indoor-scene tasks; additional tests add three task subsets sequentially. Measures are accuracy or mean average precision, chiefly validation for several datasets. Old-domain retention deteriorates with dissimilar inputs and reduced new-data coverage. Efficiency analysis compares shared forward/backward work qualitatively, without comprehensive acquisition, storage, evaluation or maintenance totals. Old training data are unnecessary for this procedure; unchanged answers everywhere are not guaranteed. Preserving teacher responses also does not establish that those responses are correct or revisable.

## What these precedents can and cannot justify

The narrow empirical warrant is that particular sequential-learning systems can reduce interference under particular task protocols. The studies distinguish protected objects and expose coverage and cost limits. They do not test an entire organization's descendants, holon fusion/fission, indefinitely changing evaluators, exact archival custody, or autonomous scientific revision. Results cannot be read as a proof that retaining more experience always produces more capability. A successor's initial ability, improvement on new work, and preservation of earlier useful ability remain separate questions.

The remaining sections are independently proposed architecture, not descriptions of mechanisms demonstrated by these papers. Numerical model losses are also different from sourced commitments, authority, or truth. An old prediction can deserve correction while the historical fact that a predecessor made it remains unchanged. Biology may suggest functions worth considering; it supplies no architectural mandate for resetting lifetimes or reproducing anatomical memory systems.

## Distinct retention targets

For ANIMA, the proposed separation is among usable competence, recoverable experience, and exact historical records. Competence includes a procedure's conditions, dependencies, interpretation and evidence of useful outcomes. Recoverable experience includes selected cases and failed or partial work that may support a later question. Exact history requires custody of the records whose original content must remain available. A model checkpoint, summary, compressed graph or executable procedure should not be advertised as reconstructing all three.

This distinction follows the [current responsibility map](../../RESPONSIBILITIES.md): TL provides attributable organization/retrieval within its release scope, while TRACE owns higher-level episodes and interpretation within its granted authority. The additional boundary supplied for this vector is explicit: current TL does **not** promise complete history after reclamation. A need for immutable original records would therefore require a deliberately chosen retention capability and scope; it cannot be inferred from the existence of TL or TRACE. This note does not inspect or qualify their implementations.

A hot graph can be costly and contested without making knowledge disposal mandatory. A world could remove an inactive interpretation from active traversal while retaining a selected record elsewhere, or discard material whose retention is not required. These are distinct choices. There is no proposal that every removed node leave a permanent active tombstone. If unresolved references or obligations need continuity, sufficient compact identity or custody information could remain outside the hot representation. A loss of backing material must not masquerade as recoverable history.

## Retaining useful behaviour while allowing correction

The proposed inheritance criterion is conditional usefulness, rather than maximum agreement with a predecessor. A successor might preserve a method valid under earlier operating conditions, adapt its use under new conditions, and reject a predecessor's incorrect generalization. Keeping the old claim attributable need not keep it operationally dominant. Conversely, calling a regression a revision would not make it legitimate: the scope of changed conditions and the evidence for changed conclusions matter.

Selective loss can concern redundant activation, obsolete assumptions, unsupported hypotheses, or costly duplicate capabilities. These are different from silently dropping a rare valid exception or an outstanding obligation. Current frequency and average performance alone would be poor reasons to erase a case whose applicability is unusual but consequential. However, retaining every possible case actively would consume attention and could preserve misleading noise. Selection should remain revisable and acknowledge uncertainty about future use.

Replay is consequently a possible means of preserving or revisiting experience, not an automatic instruction to reproduce earlier behaviour. A hypothetical rehearsal set might distinguish still-valid cases from cases requiring reinterpretation, while keeping their historical status available where needed. Generated rehearsal could expand practice cheaply but also repeat inherited mistakes. Improved average competence would not establish that rare conditions, sourcing or commitments survived. These concerns motivate coverage questions rather than a claim that a universal preservation guarantee is achievable.

## Descendants and organizational plasticity

A descendant could inherit procedures, representations, selected cases, shared tools and access relationships without copying the predecessor's whole active state. Fission might allow specialized descendants to develop different interpretations; fusion might combine complementary repertoires without resolving every disagreement into one answer. In both cases, compatible meanings and conditions of use matter as much as retained bytes. An inherited capability needs an owning participant or usable shared service; a lineage link alone cannot perform work.

Inherited task commitments should remain distinguishable from learned preferences and beliefs. A world may revise a schema without revising another participant's obligation. Organizational transformation may also change which entity can fulfill an obligation. These are responsibility questions, not gradient constraints. Normal authorized adaptation can proceed through owning participants and established evaluators; these proposals introduce no central executive or human approval ceremony for every update.

Changing representation or executor can invalidate an otherwise useful inheritance. A procedure might rely on an unavailable dependency, a predecessor's hidden interpretation, or an environment that has changed. A descendant should therefore be able to report conditional ability, degraded ability and unavailable evidence separately. No forced lifetime reset is needed to express these distinctions, but inheritance does not make unlimited competence accumulation free.

## Costs, a difficult scenario, and simpler alternatives

For later architectural comparison, proposed accounting includes original acquisition, selection, storage, retrieval, rehearsal, protected-state computation, successor adaptation, compatibility maintenance, old/new-condition assessment, and attention spent resolving regressions. Deferred records can reduce active burden while increasing retrieval and reconstruction expense. A small retained model can be cheap to serve but expensive to maintain. Missing material can force reacquisition or permanently prevent attribution. These are independent lifecycle questions; the selected training comparisons above do not answer them.

Consider a hypothetical physical-construction world that inherits an actuator calibration method. A successor gains useful expertise but encounters a new material batch and revised sensor scaling. It should preserve the method where its prior conditions still hold, investigate whether the new residuals indicate scaling error or changed material behaviour, and revise the appropriate assumption. Rehearsing earlier outputs indiscriminately could penalize the correct change. Erasing all old measurements could remove the basis for diagnosing it. Keeping every old calibration active could overwhelm the useful current distinctions. Selected original evidence, conditional methods and revisable current interpretation have different roles in this scenario.

A strong simpler alternative is shared versioned tools and conditional procedures, selected attributable cases, and capable retrieval/generation over a stable pretrained substrate. Descendants could obtain useful inheritance through access and reusable artifacts before requiring continual parameter learning. This alternative may preserve capability adequately while making revision easier to inspect. It may also miss tacit learned competence or incur costly repeated reconstruction. Those tradeoffs are hypotheses; retention machinery should earn its complexity through useful successor outcomes rather than through resemblance to a brain.

Three discriminating questions remain:

1. Does inheritance help unfamiliar successor work beyond access to the same tools and cases, while preserving useful older conditions at comparable total cost?
2. Can the successor correct a changed or false assumption without erasing the valid old conditional method, its attribution, or an applicable commitment?
3. Which rare capabilities or distinctions disappear over repeated specialization, fusion and reclamation, and can lost competence be distinguished from unavailable original evidence?
