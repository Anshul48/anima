# Optional epistemic-foraging prompts

2026-10-05. These prompts support architecture decisions; they do not authorize building products, changing contracts, buying hardware or treating a research lead as a requirement. They can be assigned independently to cheaper capable models when a question warrants more evidence. No external Muse/AGY run was launched for this packet.

A forager should receive one question, its short context and the relevant local note, rather than the entire macro conversation. Its output can live beside that note under a clearly named research subdirectory. A coordinator integrates useful findings after checking sources and scope.

## Shared instruction for each forager

Investigate the assigned architectural uncertainty using primary papers, official technical documentation and attributable demonstrations. Keep biological findings, computational analogies, implemented mechanisms and proposals separate. Distinguish an abstract-only inspection from full-methods inspection, and a published report from independent reproduction.

Prioritize sources that distinguish competing mechanisms or reveal a failure condition. A reasonable initial pass is six to ten primary sources; expand only when a specific unresolved contradiction makes another source valuable. Do not count papers or invent novelty claims as a substitute for resolving the question.

Return a compact decision memo containing: competing mechanisms; a claim/source/limitation table; the strongest counterargument; a plausible minimal mechanism; a discriminating comparison; and what the findings would change in the architecture. Link sources at the supported claim. Preserve negative or inconclusive findings. Do not convert findings into an accepted decision.

## 1. Developmental integration and inherited organization

**Question:** What existing mechanisms support useful changes to participants, representations, relationships and state custody together, beyond adding a plugin or selecting a route?

**Context:** ANIMA aims to host independently developing worlds. Co-adaptation, coalition, assimilation, fusion, fission and inheritance are relevant possibilities. Human architects and AI search can both guide changes. Read the integrated synthesis's sections on adaptation surfaces and world responsibility.

**Investigate:** Developmental encodings, learned modular structure, coordination learning, executable transformations, state migration and measures of useful integration. Look for implementations that changed internal organization and retained an advantage, not just diagrams of adaptive architecture.

**Return:** Two or three competing realizable approaches, their adaptation surfaces, what they inherit, where meaning or custody changes, and a comparison that separates reconfigurability from beneficial development. Explain which claims require a population and which can be studied within one developing system.

**Decision target:** The shared vocabulary and machinery a world needs to expose. Do not select a universal framework from conceptual similarity alone.

## 2. Whole experts, coupling and retained disagreement

**Question:** Under what conditions does persistent expertise benefit from a separate world rather than a cluster or method inside a common hierarchical memory?

**Context:** The user is open to a society of experts, but current TRACE is already hierarchical and federation is not mandatory. Source custody, interpretation, responsibility and inference are independent axes. Read the expert-worlds dossier.

**Investigate:** Evidence for persistent specialization, learning to consult, shared versus independent memory, common-mode errors, communication overhead and transfer after collaboration. Examine what makes multiple participants complementary rather than duplicated model calls.

**Return:** A coupling tradeoff map and a matched-budget comparison among a common TRACE with methods, internal specialists, and persistent expert worlds. Include a case requiring a rare counterexample and one requiring rapid shared revision.

**Decision target:** Criteria for individual expert trajectories and composite formation. Do not infer cognitive independence from process boundaries or diversity from personality labels.

## 3. Outliers, compression and prospective creativity

**Question:** How can a bounded cognitive memory discover material that its summaries or current organization fail to represent?

**Context:** Warm/cold evidence can be recoverable while the hot graph is costly and lossy. The user's concern is useful combinations of outliers from distant holons. Read the outlier sections and creativity dossier.

**Investigate:** Multiple representatives, residual representations, counterexample indexes, alternative groupings, bridge discovery, replay and novelty preservation. Separate improvements in ordinary retrieval from improvements in valid novel solutions.

**Return:** A mechanism portfolio with total costs and failure modes. Propose held-out tasks requiring cross-region exceptions or new compositions, with controls for leakage, hindsight and evaluator noise. Identify what artificial planted examples can and cannot establish.

**Decision target:** Which discovery routes deserve an explicit allowance and how to avoid treating novelty as evidence of usefulness.

## 4. Context selection and commitment continuity

**Question:** What combination of selective context, explicit state and limited checks improves long-horizon behavior without freezing old mistakes or suppressing legitimate novelty?

**Context:** 4C asks for relevant information at consequential micro-steps and consistency across long histories. Governing limits, task commitments, beliefs and learned schema preferences must be distinct. Read the creativity-and-constraints dossier.

**Investigate:** Working-memory gating as a functional inspiration; computational relevance selection; dependency tracking; authorized revision; formal coverage; semantic judge failure; and cases where a commitment's scope changes.

**Return:** A proposed separation of responsibilities, uncertain applicability cases, and a comparison measuring violations, missed exceptions, false blocks and recovery costs. Include failure cases where a perfect record preserves the wrong interpretation.

**Decision target:** What should be mechanically enforced, what needs domain interpretation and what should trigger further evidence. Avoid claiming a universal anatomical controller or complete natural-language consistency.

## 5. Artificial-muscle co-design and useful simulation

**Question:** Which combinations of morphology, transmission, actuation, sensing and control deserve further investigation for capable hands, and what simulation fidelity would discriminate them?

**Context:** The user prefers biomimetic and artificial-muscle designs, permits conventional devices as a necessary bootstrap, and wants a hardware construction ecosystem rather than only a robot demo. Read the physical-development dossier.

**Investigate:** Installed-system force, stroke, speed, power delivery, heat, durability, packaging, controllability, manufacturing and maintenance. Separate tendon morphology and passive compliance from actuator energy conversion. Identify tractable surrogates and the measurements needed to calibrate them.

**Return:** A small tradeoff map, two contrasting mechanisms, sources for the critical assumptions and a falsification plan. Show which rankings change under plausible uncertainty. Do not pick a humanoid body or purchase plan from component performance alone.

**Decision target:** The early work envelope, interfaces that must remain revisable, and which physical information is worth acquiring next.

## 6. Construction languages and cheap policies

**Question:** Can a growing repertoire of executable construction operations improve completed domain work and future reuse, with cheap selection inside that repertoire?

**Context:** 4A uses frontend as an example. Jev is a possible non-generative selector, not a generative builder or proof system. The operation catalogue must have an escape path when a task needs a new formulation. Read the construction-language note.

**Investigate:** Library learning, program synthesis, domain-specific construction operators, applicability checks, menu completeness, state representation, visual evidence and prospective abstraction from partial failures. Audit whole-system costs rather than advertised output-token prices.

**Return:** A repertoire/policy co-development mechanism and a comparison against a capable generative baseline and an authored-operator baseline. Include changed option names and tasks beyond the initial repertoire. Measure actual integrated outcomes and held-out reuse.

**Decision target:** Whether reusable operations should be authored, learned, compiled, adapted into models or combined—and which claim deserves the first bounded test.

## Reconciliation rule

Different foragers can return incompatible mechanisms without one being wrong. Preserve their assumptions and predicted consequences, identify the decision each could change, and request further evidence only where the disagreement is consequential. A source-supported analogy is a lead; an implemented mechanism is scoped evidence; a result on a controlled task is neither a qualified product nor a proof of general intelligence.

These research tasks can run asynchronously alongside current delivery. Their findings should inform deliberate future choices, rather than create an ever-growing prerequisite chain before useful work proceeds.
