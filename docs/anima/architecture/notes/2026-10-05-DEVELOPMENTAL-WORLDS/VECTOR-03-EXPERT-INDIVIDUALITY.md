# Vector 3 — Expert individuality and a developing society

Architecture discussion, 2026-10-06. This is the full third-vector treatment in the current round. It builds on [developmental change](VECTOR-01-DEVELOPMENTAL-CHANGE.md), [attention and commitments](VECTOR-02-ATTENTION-AND-COMMITMENTS.md), and the [expert-worlds dossier](EXPERT-WORLDS-AND-SEARCH.md). Recommendations remain proposals while the user reads the round.

**My current bet:** preserve different learning trajectories where they produce useful competence, and allow their boundaries to change as cooperation develops. A society of experts should be able to acquire shared machinery, form composites, separate, and consolidate discoveries into common capabilities.

The important question is where persistent specialization earns its cost. Neither one enormous TRACE nor a permanently separated collection of TRACE instances should be assumed to answer that question.

The user's ambition is larger than an agent ensemble. A participant can contain models, memories, tools, representations and procedures; repeated interaction can change several of those together. Expertise can become a property of a participant, a relationship or a composite. The substrate needs to accommodate all three.

## The hypothesis we are actually considering

There are several claims hiding inside “specialized models working in concert.”

One concerns inference: different models may have complementary abilities. Another concerns knowledge organization: different regions can make relevant evidence and methods easier to activate. A third concerns development: persistent participants can accumulate different histories, representations and working practices. A fourth concerns integration: repeated cooperation can produce a capability beyond occasional consultation.

These claims can succeed or fail separately. Better routing does not establish beneficial persistent individuality. A successful ensemble does not establish useful fusion. A composite working on familiar tasks does not establish transfer or general intelligence.

The most ambitious hypothesis here is that **cooperating developmental units can build and retain useful cognitive organization**. That could happen within one hierarchical TRACE, across several TRACE worlds, or through a mixture whose boundaries evolve.

Current TRACE is already hierarchical. Calling it monolithic would obscure the actual choice. We are considering how much functional independence its specialists need, which state they share, and when a new unit should acquire its own trajectory.

## What makes a specialist an expert?

A topic label says where material belongs. Expertise concerns what a system can do with material, including unfamiliar demands and changed conditions.

An expert should be assessable on a task family: how it frames problems, retrieves relevant evidence, selects methods, recognizes applicability limits, develops alternatives and responds to outcomes. Competence can be narrow, advisory or emerging. It need not imply authority to act.

A persistent expert world adds a place for that competence to develop. Its relevant state might include interpretations, methods, experiments, unresolved questions, successful procedures and failure lessons. Some expertise can also reside in model parameters or tools hosted elsewhere.

| Arrangement | What it can provide | What remains to establish |
|---|---|---|
| Domain region or cluster | Organized access to relevant material and associated operations | Whether its organization improves the work |
| Specialist invoked for a task | A distinct inference or working process | Whether it contributes beyond another sample or method call |
| Persistent expert world | A continuing trajectory of interpretation, method and feedback | Whether persistence improves future capability enough to justify its cost |
| Expert relationship or composite | Shared representations, procedures and joint experience | Whether cooperation creates reusable competence and survives changed demands |

These are roles, not permanent species. A cluster can contain the state and machinery of an expert world. A world can host several experts. A composite can become an expert in its own right.

A separate directory, process, model or TRACE installation is therefore neither necessary nor sufficient for individuality. Useful distinctions can live inside shared infrastructure. Conversely, separate infrastructure can contain several copies of the same effective method.

## A developmental unit, without demanding complete autonomy

I would treat expert individuality as a consequential continuing trajectory, rather than a claim that the participant is self-sufficient.

The useful questions are whether its experience changes future work, whether it can retain a different interpretation, and whether its adaptation can proceed without forcing unrelated participants to change identically.

It can share an inference model, source store, scheduler or experimental facility. It may depend on another expert's methods. It may be inactive between tasks. An advisory expert can have a valuable trajectory without controlling any shared operational state.

The larger society can also include human experts, organizations, robots and experimental facilities. Their contributions and responsibilities can be represented without assuming that their judgment or internal state has been fully digitized. A computational expert world can host part of that collaboration while depending on participants beyond its boundary.

Persistent identity helps relate earlier and later arrangements. It does not by itself create expertise. A recognized participant must have some attributable continuity in the organization that performs the work.

That continuity also need not preserve the original participant forever. Its methods may become a common primitive. Its state may move into a composite. A later split may produce successors that differ substantially from the original experts.

The developmental unit is a useful locus of change and experience. It need not be an indivisible computational person.

## When independence might earn its cost

Several reasons would justify preserving a distinct trajectory.

An expert may retain distinctions that shared consolidation repeatedly loses. It may learn a method that complements the dominant approach. It may adapt to a changing environment without making every other participant adopt that interpretation. Its continuing history may reduce the cost of reconstructing useful experience.

The claim should be connected to behavior. A participant called “reliability expert” is valuable if its methods recover consequential failure conditions, improve an intervention or develop a better alternative. Maintaining a separate voice is insufficient.

There are also reasons to consolidate. Two experts may repeatedly duplicate retrieval, retain equivalent interpretations and propose the same methods. Their continuing separation may add reconciliation without preserving a useful difference. A common procedure could retain their useful contribution more economically.

Independence is therefore a design variable. It can increase, decrease or shift to another part of the organization.

A specialist might begin as a local region, develop persistent methods, acquire a separate responsibility, then become part of a composite. Another might remain an inexpensive capability shared by many worlds. Neither trajectory should be prescribed merely by its domain name.

## The strongest simpler alternative

The serious comparison is one hierarchical TRACE with overlapping specialist regions, attributed interpretations, persistent local histories, learned routing and a capable model pool.

Such a system could preserve much of the proposed society's useful individuality while avoiding separate deployments and duplicated stores. It could keep unresolved alternatives in a shared representation. It could allocate different methods to the same question.

We should give this alternative comparable learning opportunities, source access and counterexample retrieval. Otherwise, a society wins because its baseline was denied the mechanisms that made it useful.

If two arrangements contain equivalent cognitive mechanisms, moving one into separate runtimes should be evaluated as a placement or isolation change. It would not establish a gain from expert individuality.

There can still be practical reasons for distributed instances: concurrent work, distinct custody, different hardware, specialized model training or independent lifecycle. Those advantages should remain distinguishable from claims about reasoning.

My bet concerns differentiated development with adjustable coupling. It leaves room for an effective society whose individuality largely lives inside shared machinery.

## Sharing is several different decisions

“Let the experts share knowledge” is too broad to determine an architecture.

| What is shared | Potential benefit | Important distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Source evidence | Common access and less duplicate storage | Referring to the same source does not make interpretations independent corroboration |
| Interpretations | Easier coordination and reuse | A joint conclusion can differ from a participant's continuing assessment |
| Procedures | Reusable methods and less reconstruction | Applicability and failures must survive the export |
| Intermediate state | Faster joint reasoning and experimentation | Meaning, update rules and omissions matter |
| Models or adapters | Common inference and transferable learning | Shared training can synchronize strengths and errors |
| Resources and facilities | Efficient use of compute, tools and experiments | Local allowances do not create additional shared resources |
| Commitments | Coherent external behavior | Shared responsibilities need actual custodians and revision semantics |

Shared evidence with different interpretation is a useful candidate arrangement. It is not automatically the optimum: interpretation, indexing and reconciliation still cost work, and some participants need different access or custody.

A joint representation can retain competing explanations, uncertainties and exceptions. Private copies are not the only means of protecting diversity.

The harder problem is whether a minority distinction remains expressible, discoverable and usable as shared machinery develops. Keeping its original transcript somewhere does not establish that protection.

## What crosses an expert boundary?

An expert can export a result, a method, evidence, uncertainty, intermediate state, an operation or access to a working process. The appropriate form depends on what the recipient needs.

A recipient may need only a compact result for a familiar exact operation. A novel research question may require the assumptions behind the result, alternative explanations and a way to inspect the underlying experiment.

The relationship should be able to learn which exchanges help. It can develop translations, specialized representations, shared workspaces or joint operators. Some recurring work may migrate into the relationship itself.

There still needs to be enough common operational meaning to use the exchange coherently. A typed message does not establish semantic compatibility. A learned translation can omit conditions. A successful familiar exchange can conceal a private convention that fails for a new caller.

That common floor need not impose one universal ontology or permanent semantic API. Participants can negotiate or learn richer arrangements above it. Consequential losses and unresolved interpretation should remain assessable.

The communication objective also changes during a task. Early exploration may need distinct formulations. Later construction may need a precise shared intermediate artifact. Testing may require independent assessments before comparison.

A permanently maximal communication channel would not preserve every advantage of separation. It might cause premature convergence, increase context costs or make local adaptation harder to attribute.

## A worked trajectory: materials and control developing together

Consider a hypothetical construction world investigating a soft tendon-driven hand component. This is an illustrative task, not a selected robot design or hardware programme.

A materials expert maintains methods for interpreting deformation, fatigue and environmental sensitivity. A control expert maintains methods for estimating state, choosing interventions and assessing behavior. Both can use shared experimental records while retaining different questions.

Initially, they exchange reports. The materials expert supplies a simplified relation between load and displacement. The control expert uses it in a candidate controller.

A repeated failure reveals the limitation of that exchange. The controller's own actuation history changes the component's response. A report that omits that history becomes inadequate, even if its fitted values were correct for the original experiment.

Several developments can follow:

1. The materials expert changes its model to include relevant loading history.
2. The control expert changes its state estimate and experiment selection.
3. Their relationship acquires a shared representation of history and observed response.
4. They develop a joint procedure that chooses experiments informative for both questions.
5. Repeated useful work produces a persistent composite capability for investigating this class of components.

The important innovation is more than a stronger communication edge. Both participants' methods changed, and part of the useful computation moved into shared machinery.

One possible branch goes further. The composite takes custody of the joint model, its update procedure and the experimental queue. It becomes responsible for the investigation capability exported to callers, including the conditions attached to its results. Materials analysis and control become internal roles. Their old boundary between separately maintained model reports and controller updates is replaced by a joint state-update process. Continuing experiments, resource allocations and unresolved claims now belong to this new operational arrangement.

Later, a changed task motivates separation. A successor materials world receives the relevant model state and access to the joint experimental records; a successor control world receives the estimation procedures and declared model dependencies. The still-running experiment is assigned to a named custodian rather than silently restarted by both. Outstanding external commitments are allocated or remain with a residual composite until fulfilled. Shared allocations are divided under their existing limits.

Some jointly learned state may remain in a shared capability because it cannot yet be decomposed usefully. A successor might need to reacquire calibration under its new conditions or develop a translation for the state it inherited. These successors have an attributable lineage; they are not assumed to reproduce the original experts. The separation has changed actual custody, operation and responsibility, while preserving the distinction between inherited methods, supporting evidence and unresolved interpretation.

At either stage, a newly introduced manufacturing expert may need conditions the current arrangement previously ignored. Its questions can reveal that the working representation is too narrow. Cooperation may develop again, rather than forcing every caller through a permanently frozen interface.

The outcome still needs evaluation. The pair might have built an excellent procedure for one component that transfers poorly. It might have reduced visible errors by avoiding difficult regimes. It might rely on a convention that a successor cannot reconstruct.

A capable single construction world equipped with the same methods might discover an equally good arrangement. The example establishes a possible developmental path, not superiority of a distributed implementation.

## Expertise can reside between participants

In the worked trajectory, neither expert need possess the entire joint capability. Part of it can reside in the coupled representation, experiment policy and history.

This is a stronger form of distributed cognition than dividing a task into independent subtasks and collecting answers. The decomposition itself changes through experience.

It has an important consequence for expert extraction. We cannot assume that copying a domain subgraph or a participant's local history reproduces its useful competence. Some competence depends on relationships, tools, shared state or an environment that the extracted unit no longer has.

Extraction should therefore ask what must accompany the participant, what can remain remotely accessible, and what has to be reacquired through interaction. A smaller, partly capable successor may be useful without pretending it preserves everything.

A composite also need not have one universal understanding of all its constituents. It can export a scoped capability while retaining internal differences and dependencies.

The substrate's promise is to make these organizations possible and their consequences intelligible. Discovering which organizations help is the separate learning problem.

## Deep integration and useful difference can coexist

Fusion does not require every interpretation to collapse into one answer. Shared state can contain attributable alternatives. A composite can support different methods and invite a minority expert when its conditions become relevant.

Conversely, preserving every old distinction indefinitely can prevent useful abstraction. A disagreement can be resolved through evidence. An obsolete method can be retired. A capability can become shared machinery when individual custody no longer adds value.

The goal is to preserve differences with prospective utility, alongside enough history to reconsider their retirement when conditions change.

Retained usefulness and epistemic endorsement are separate. The system may preserve a supported finding, an unresolved hypothesis, a useful method or question, or a contradicted interpretation retained to explain past work. A procedure succeeding under particular conditions does not validate its author's wider explanation. Fusion and inheritance should preserve these statuses rather than promote everything retained into authoritative knowledge.

That utility is uncertain. A rare method may look dispensable until a new task exposes its value. A noisy distinction may consume attention without helping. The architecture should support comparisons and recovery routes where feasible, rather than promising a perfect retention policy.

A sensible organization can combine tightly coupled local computation with wider independent trajectories. It can use temporary coalitions for unfamiliar problems and consolidate recurring arrangements.

The coupling should be task-sensitive. Some intermediate operations benefit from direct, frequent exchange. Others benefit from independent investigation before conclusions are compared.

Logical individuality need not introduce a remote message boundary into every interaction. Compatible participants might share structured intermediate state, numerical representations or a joint simulator; supported adaptation interfaces could expose richer learning signals. These are possible coupling mechanisms, not claims that arbitrary model internals can already be combined. Their usefulness depends on meaning, compatibility and the actual computation and communication costs.

## Fusion, separation and retirement need real accounting

A composite can acquire shared state, procedures and responsibilities. A split needs to determine which successors receive or can access those things.

Jointly developed machinery may no longer decompose along the original boundaries. Restoring two old identifiers does not restore the original competence. A successor may need a translated representation, retained joint capability or renewed developmental work.

The operation should also preserve relevant obligation meaning. Participant dissolution does not discharge a continuing commitment. Splitting one allowance does not duplicate a shared budget. These are continuity questions developed in Vector 2.

Retirement has several forms: reduced activation, removal of a rebuildable projection, transfer into a common procedure, archival of local state, or termination of a participant. Their consequences differ.

The user's preference for a costly and contested active graph remains compatible with persistent experts. Persistence need not mean permanent occupancy of the hot graph. Current useful state can stay compact; historical inquiry can pay for deeper retrieval.

Records that explain old work need not participate in ordinary cognitive traversal. Recovery nevertheless depends on source availability and meaningful pointers. A reference to discarded material cannot support an unlimited promise of reconstructability.

Useful forgetting and useful inheritance must be designed together. The system should know which capabilities depend on retained history and which can be rebuilt more cheaply.

## Cognitive diversity should affect the work

The user's interest in psychological and emotional profiles is a possible source of research hypotheses. Human trait labels do not by themselves specify a computational mechanism, and a different writing style does not establish different expertise.

I would begin with observable working tendencies:

| Variation | What to examine |
|---|---|
| Abstraction and concrete exemplars | Which distinctions are retained and when a generalization fails |
| Analogy and decomposition | Which candidate formulations become available |
| Intervention and observational analysis | Which evidence is sought and which assumptions are challenged |
| Breadth and depth | Coverage of alternatives versus refinement of promising ones |
| Counterexample seeking | Discovery of conditions that undermine a favored result |
| Tolerance for unresolved alternatives | Whether uncertainty survives long enough to support better investigation |

These tendencies can be induced through prompts, tools, procedures, training or different developmental experience. Prompted behavior is a legitimate experimental condition; it is not proof that a persistent psychological identity exists.

The dimensions also need not be fixed personalities. An expert can learn when a method is useful, and a coalition can allocate several methods without assigning each forever to a separate participant.

A useful comparison would inspect what the participants actually retrieve, propose, test and retain. Evaluate complementary competence and failure patterns, including changed demands and later reuse.

A system producing more unusual ideas can still contribute less. A cautious system can preserve an exception or waste effort avoiding feasible work. The value lies in the selected or composed outcome after the associated costs.

I would resist requiring every expert to resemble a human professional. Some useful participants may combine methods humans rarely combine or operate as narrowly scoped, highly capable procedures.

## Why many voices can reproduce one error

Agreement is strongest when the assessments contribute meaningfully distinct evidence or methods. Different names, histories or models do not automatically provide that distinction.

Experts can share a faulty source interpretation, a common evaluator, a translation that drops an exception, or training that produces similar errors. A second assessment can improve scrutiny without qualifying as independent confirmation.

A simple mathematical illustration makes the limitation visible. Suppose \(n\) estimators have centered scalar errors \(\epsilon_i\), each with variance \(\sigma^2\), and every distinct pair has correlation \(\rho\). For \(n>1\), a feasible equicorrelation covariance requires \(-1/(n-1)\leq\rho\leq1\). Then:

\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_i\right)
=
\frac{\sigma^2}{n}\bigl(1+(n-1)\rho\bigr).
\]

This follows by adding the individual variances and pairwise covariances. If errors are uncorrelated, averaging reduces this variance by a factor of \(n\). Perfectly correlated errors provide no such reduction. A shared bias is not removed by this argument either.

This is an illustrative equal-variance estimation model, not a theorem about LLM voting, creative search or expert societies. Its lesson is to inspect common error structure rather than counting agreeing participants.

Complementarity also depends on the task. Two experts can have overlapping weaknesses on routine work yet different strengths on rare conditions. Aggregate correlation can hide that structure.

An arrangement needs a suitable way to use its contributions. If no evaluator, experiment or decision process can distinguish useful alternatives, adding proposals may increase uncertainty or exploit measurement noise.

## A society need not hold a discussion about everything

The architecture should support several modes of cooperation.

Routine work can use an established operator or procedure. Independent exploration can proceed until an intermediate result is worth sharing. A demanding coupled operation can use a common working representation. A consequential unresolved interpretation can receive targeted consultation.

The system can learn when these modes help. It can also learn which experts to consult, which state to expose and when to stop consulting.

A coordinator should not become the sole mind reconstructing every specialist's context. Local responsibility, coherent exports and shared artifacts can reduce that burden. Several coordinating arrangements can coexist at different scales.

Coordination has its own limits. Repeated handoffs can consume more work than the underlying task. Experts can learn to request excessive context, defer hard questions or optimize an easy local metric. More orderly cooperation can still reduce overall capability.

The system's feedback should include completed work, consequential omissions, unnecessary interaction, reconstruction cost and later reuse. Local reward and system benefit need not coincide.

Human attention is part of that cost. A society should improve its ability to identify the few clarifications that change consequential choices, while continuing work under established direction. Delegating a question to another model does not justify repeatedly returning the same uncertainty to the user.

## Several timescales of individuality

Fast cooperation can share activated representations and intermediate state. Task-level cooperation can accumulate experiments and candidate formulations. Slower development can revise procedures, representations and organization.

Inheritance can then preserve selected methods, state, construction rules or access to existing capabilities. These transfers are further transformations with their own applicability conditions.

The timescales can overlap. An expert may be learning while a coalition operates. A newly discovered method may become reusable before the task ends. A long-lived composite can contain temporary specialist arrangements.

This matters for the user's larger vision. A society need not wait for a full evolutionary population before it develops useful organization. Persistent specialization and co-adaptation can be investigated sooner; population selection and richer inheritance can build on the resulting machinery.

Neither sustained activity nor self-modification guarantees cumulative improvement. The relevant question is what useful capability persists and transfers after the work.

## The trillion-token ambition

Trillions of tokens can mean an archive, aggregate lifetime computation, simultaneous activity across participants, or an attempted single coherent reasoning episode. Those are different claims.

A society can distribute retrieval and investigation without making every token part of one argument. It can retain partial results and procedures for later composition. That is a plausible route to cumulative work beyond one model's active context.

Coherent reasoning across that work still requires meaningful dependencies, accountable revisions, relevant evidence and suitable evaluation. A large archive does not automatically supply a useful world model. Aggregate generation does not establish a retained capability.

The desired gain may therefore be a succession of better representations and methods, rather than one ever-longer internal monologue.

A persistent expert can make previous computation easier to reuse. A relationship can make composition cheaper. A shared primitive can reduce future reconstruction. These are concrete ways that cumulative work might improve the economics of future reasoning.

Whether they produce broadly stronger intelligence remains an empirical question. We should evaluate reusable improvement on new demands instead of treating organizational scale as its proxy.

## What the primary literature supports

Hong and Page model functional diversity through representations and search methods. Their theorem depends on restrictive conditions, including the availability of a solver that can improve each nonoptimal state and a group process that reaches a common stopping point. This motivates method diversity, not an unconditional claim that heterogeneous teams outperform stronger individuals. The framework and theorem sections of the author-hosted paper were inspected. [PNAS paper, 2004](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/scottepage/wp-content/uploads/sites/344/2015/11/pnas.pdf)

Kirkpatrick and colleagues investigate retention during sequential neural-network learning by protecting weights important to earlier tasks. The primary abstract describes classification and Atari demonstrations. It supports treating learned capability retention as a mechanism requiring evidence. It does not establish that separate expert worlds are necessary, or that external-memory retirement is the same problem as neural weight interference. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)

The separate [bounded evidence note](VECTOR-03-EVIDENCE-NOTE.md) examines contemporary multi-agent claims and comparison limits. Its inspected scope and source revisions should govern how those findings are used.

These are research leads. No experiment was reproduced in this discussion, and no cited result validates the proposed developmental society as a whole.

## What would discriminate the hypothesis?

The comparison should separate specialization, persistence, cooperation and placement. A single “one agent versus many agents” score cannot tell us which contributed.

Give competing arrangements comparable evidence, model pools, tools and opportunities for learning. Include a capable hierarchical TRACE alternative, repeated sampling or search, temporary specialists, persistent expert trajectories and learned composite arrangements where appropriate.

A baseline should be allowed to organize its work well. If its mechanisms amount to expert individuality inside shared infrastructure, that is a substantive finding about where the boundary belongs.

Use repeated task families with changing conditions. Include ordinary cases, rare exceptions, missing methods, conflicting interpretations and problems requiring several domains. Later tasks should require prospective reuse rather than replaying the original solved example.

Track complete costs: discovery, inference, retrieval, synchronization, interpretation repair, state maintenance, experiments and human attention. Report outcomes and costs separately where converting everything into one utility score would hide important requirements.

Useful discriminating questions include:

- Does retaining a trajectory improve later work beyond retaining the same sources?
- Do distinct methods improve the selected or composed result beyond extra sampling?
- Does cooperation create a reusable operation or only an expensive familiar-task convention?
- Does a new caller understand and use the acquired capability?
- Does a changed task recover useful minority competence?
- Can a successor function after separation, with the retained or reacquired dependencies declared?
- Does consolidation preserve the capability while reducing maintenance?
- Can the system recognize an expertise gap and investigate or request a targeted clarification?

Ablations should preserve coherent alternatives. Deleting history without restoring access to necessary evidence tests information deprivation. It does not isolate the effect of individuality. Likewise, cutting all exchange tests a disconnected system rather than the benefit of a different cooperation policy.

Some controlled cases can test mechanics. Realistic workflows and later transfer are needed to assess whether the resulting organization does useful work outside planted scenarios.

The evaluation machinery remains fallible. Open-ended insight may lack a quick oracle. The system can accumulate evidence, test consequences and distinguish unresolved claims; more expert agreement cannot substitute for an absent evaluation method.

## A demanding but bounded architectural comparison

The hypothetical materials-and-control trajectory offers one suitable shape of investigation.

Begin with complementary methods and shared records. Compare a capable integrated arrangement with differentiated trajectories. Let repeated failures inform adaptation. Assess whether a shared model or procedure emerges, then introduce a changed component or a new caller.

Finally, examine consolidation and separation. Determine what useful state survives, which dependency remains and whether successors can continue without reconstructing all prior work.

This would exercise useful individuality, joint development and transferable integration within one bounded task family. It would not establish robot capability, artificial life, general intelligence or a necessary route to them.

The experiment is proposed, not launched. A software-only domain could supply the same organizational test when its outcomes are easier to assess; the task should follow the uncertainty being investigated.

The important design target is a system whose boundaries can change without losing the meaning of what developed.

## What I would preserve in the macro architecture

These proposals inherit the current [responsibility map](../../RESPONSIBILITIES.md) and [operative direction](../../../context/OPERATIVE-BRIEF.md). TL supplies its attributable source organization and retrieval; TRACE owns its scoped interpretation and history responsibilities; SST supplies bounded exploration with domain evaluation retained by the responsible world. STC is a construction and execution world, and the broad substrate supports hosting, resources, lifecycle and organizational change. Expert-world design can build on those capabilities while their owning contracts govern present work. Additional communication arrangements for the proposed society are a separate question from existing project protocol decisions.

I propose five principles for later consideration.

1. **Expert individuality concerns a useful developmental trajectory.** It can exist within shared infrastructure and need not imply complete autonomy.
2. **Sharing and separation have several axes.** Sources, interpretations, procedures, models, state, resources and commitments can have different boundaries.
3. **Relationships and composites can acquire expertise.** Integration may change both participants and create machinery that does not belong to either alone.
4. **Diversity is assessed through complementary work and errors.** Preserve useful differences while allowing evidence-based consolidation and retirement.
5. **Organization remains plastic.** Extraction, fusion, separation and inheritance account for capabilities, dependencies, commitments, state and costs.

I would leave the number of experts, their domain divisions, communication protocol, runtime topology, psychological labels and model allocation open. Those choices should follow actual tasks and learning evidence.

The architectural ambition is a society that can become more capable by developing both its participants and their forms of cooperation. The small shared foundation enables that freedom; the usefulness of each resulting organization must still be established.
