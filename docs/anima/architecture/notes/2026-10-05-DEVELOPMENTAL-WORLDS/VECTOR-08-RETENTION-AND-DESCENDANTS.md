# Vector 8 — Retention and descendants

> Public curation note: links to records excluded from this public release were converted to plaintext historical references; the original record identities are preserved inline.

2026-10-07. **Status: macro-architecture proposals and bounded primary-source analysis, for discussion.** Advancing through this vector round does not accept its recommendations or authorize implementation.

**My central proposal:** a successor should inherit useful organization, the conditions under which it helps, and the means to revise or replace it. Retention should make future capability cheaper to develop while keeping active organization selective.

This brings the earlier vectors together. Experience can change representations, procedures, relationships, expert organization and embodiment. The remaining question is what survives those changes, in what form, and for whose future work.

Your starting commitments matter here. Space in the working graph should be costly and contested. Retired bodies should leave it. Historical investigation should bear its actual retrieval and reconstruction costs. At the same time, valuable expertise should not have to be learned again merely because its current activation ended.

These commitments are compatible. They require us to distinguish ending activation, retiring a current interpretation, retaining a capability, preserving historical evidence and transferring responsibility.

The ambitious target is cumulative development across worlds and generations of work. A descendant should be able to begin farther along without being forced to preserve the ancestor's whole organization.

## What counts as a successor?

A successor can arise in several ways.

A continuing world may replace a controller, representation or model while retaining its work and responsibilities. A new world may start from selected machinery developed elsewhere. A composite may form from previously separate participants. A split can produce new specialists whose competence depends on shared machinery acquired before the split. A physical design can be realized in a new specimen.

These cases involve different continuity claims.

The world that continues an unfinished experiment needs to understand effects already enacted and results still pending. A new laboratory borrowing its diagnostic method might need neither the experiment's unfinished work nor its authority. A copied controller does not make a new hand the same physical specimen.

A descendant can also have several predecessors. Its procedure may come from one world, its representation from another, and its measurement method from a shared facility. Some contributions remain accessible services rather than copied possessions.

I would therefore use lineage to explain contributions and transformations. Lineage need not impose one parent, one ownership tree or one permanent anatomical hierarchy.

Several things should remain distinguishable: ancestry, source identity, runtime identity, state custody, current competence and granted authority. A historical contribution can remain attributable after its contributor stops operating. A new identity can continue an obligation. An unchanged identifier can conceal a substantial loss of competence.

This follows the [responsibility map](../../RESPONSIBILITIES.md). It also preserves the wider canvas: descendants can be expert societies, construction worlds, tools, learned models or physical arrangements. STC is one important construction setting.

## What can be inherited?

The inherited object is broader than a code artifact or a model checkpoint.

| Contribution | Possible benefit to a successor | What still needs to be understood |
|---|---|---|
| A source or observation | Earlier evidence can inform a new question | Its conditions, provenance and continued availability |
| An interpretation or schema | Relevant distinctions become cheaper to access | Its scope, alternatives, exceptions and unresolved meaning |
| A procedure or executable capability | Familiar work can be performed with less reconstruction | Applicability, dependencies, actual effects and recovery |
| A learned parameter state or policy | Acquired behavior can continue | Model compatibility, retained competence and adaptation under changed demands |
| A developed relationship or composite | Coordination or joint expertise can continue | Which participants, representations, resources and shared state make it work |
| A developmental or learning rule | A new environment can construct an appropriate capability | Required observations, costs, failure conditions and what it actually generates |
| A prepared environment or physical arrangement | Later work starts with useful scaffolding | Custody, physical condition, calibration and availability |

These are discussion categories. They are not seven required services or a universal inheritance schema.

They can overlap. A learned controller may rely on a representation, a calibration method and a particular sensor. A relationship may include an executable translator, a shared uncertainty model and an established division of work.

The relevant unit is whatever makes the claimed contribution usable. The architecture should allow that unit to be investigated and revised.

Copying the smallest artifact can omit necessary context. Copying the largest available world can import obsolete assumptions and costly machinery. Both can fail.

## Learned state and inherited state should be distinguishable

Within a world, adaptation changes its current state. When a successor is formed, some contribution is selected, transformed or made accessible.

Those are different operations even when they happen close together.

A world might learn a highly local calibration during one task. A descendant could receive that calibration, a method for acquiring its own calibration, or only the finding that this class of controller is sensitive to a particular measurement condition.

It could receive all three with different statuses.

The distinction between acquired state and heritable contribution is useful computationally. It does not require copying a biological restriction on transmitting lifetime learning. Software can deliberately pass acquired parameters and procedures to another instance.

We also need not impose biological generations on every participant. A long-lived world can develop continuously while periodically exporting reusable methods. Other worlds can start, split, combine or retire on different timescales.

Variation, selection and inheritance are relevant future mechanisms. Useful developmental change can be studied before a complete reproductive ecology exists. The [first vector](VECTOR-01-DEVELOPMENTAL-CHANGE.md) already distinguishes permitting change from discovering changes that improve capability.

There is no need to force descendants to begin with weak priors merely to make the process resemble evolution.

## A snapshot, a recipe and a learning rule do different work

A snapshot preserves a particular starting state. It can be valuable when continued operation depends on acquired details that are expensive to reconstruct.

A recipe describes how to construct an arrangement. It can be valuable when a mature snapshot contains accidental local choices or cannot be instantiated in the new setting.

A learning rule guides adaptation through interaction. It can be valuable when the appropriate arrangement depends on observations that are unavailable before development begins.

None dominates in all cases.

A copied expert can respond immediately but carry a stale representation. A concise developmental recipe can be portable but require substantial training and evaluation before it becomes competent. A learned adaptation rule can fail when its new inputs differ in a consequential way.

One primary precedent makes the distinction concrete. Andrychowicz and colleagues learn an optimizer, then use it to train new problem instances. Their published experiments inspect particular transfers across network sizes and tasks, while a sigmoid-to-ReLU change defeats generalization in the MNIST example. Optimizer training itself uses an outer optimization process and validation. This supports investigating inherited adaptation machinery with explicit scope and acquisition costs; it does not establish a general rule for developing cognitive worlds. Sections 2–3 of the published paper were inspected. [Published NeurIPS paper, 2016](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2016/file/fb87582825f9d28a8d42c5e5e5e8b23d-Paper.pdf)

For ANIMA, a possible contribution is a rule that notices recurring disagreements between predicted and observed outcomes, investigates alternative explanations, and develops a shared diagnostic representation where useful. Different descendants could enact different organizations through that rule.

That is a substantive developmental hypothesis. A stored instruction saying “improve your architecture” is too underspecified to establish it.

A mixture can be more useful: a small working capability, evidence about its limits, and a method for extending it.

## Inheritance can preserve a coupled arrangement

Some useful organization belongs to an interaction.

Suppose an interpretation world and a construction world develop a shared distinction between requested, reported and observed effects. Their consultation policy and diagnostic procedure evolve around that distinction. Later, each world alone may no longer contain the competence they express together.

A descendant can inherit the whole arrangement, access it as a maintained participant, or acquire a transformed contribution that supports a different cooperation.

Splitting the arrangement requires understanding what has become shared. Restoring the original participant names does not restore their original independence.

This is where the endosymbiosis inspiration remains relevant at the architectural level. Integration can change what the participants maintain, which mechanisms they depend on and where a capability resides. No exact biological reconstruction is assumed.

The practical question is whether inheriting that integration helps further work. Greater dependence is not itself evidence of improvement. A loose arrangement may adapt better to component failures or new conditions.

The system should permit both stronger integration and continued independence. Selection among them should follow actual consequences.

## A society inherits through its environment too

A descendant's starting advantage can live outside the descendant.

A shared archive, simulator, instrument, training curriculum, repertoire or expert facility can preserve earlier work. A new participant may learn faster because the surrounding world supplies examples, assessment and collaboration that the ancestor had to construct.

This means a lineage can improve its developmental environment as well as its participants.

It also means evaluating only the size of a transmitted artifact can be misleading. A tiny seed that depends on an expensive maintained expert service has not eliminated that service's cost.

The distinction is especially important for your society-of-experts idea. Reuse can occur through shared machinery, differentiated learning trajectories and relationships among them. We should not require every expert to copy a complete TRACE or load the society's knowledge into its own context.

Nor should we label a group more capable merely because it inherits more compute, access or prepared evidence. Those advantages are legitimate resources, but the comparison needs to account for them.

A promising research target is **improving the environment in which later capabilities develop**: better diagnostic cases, useful representations, construction operations, coordination methods and assessment. That can produce cumulative leverage without one model accumulating every capability internally.

## Historical preservation and competence retention answer different questions

Historical preservation asks what can be recovered about earlier material and events.

Competence retention asks what the system can still do, at what cost, under which conditions.

A faithful transcript can support historical analysis while leaving a successor unable to reproduce the former skill. A working skill can perform well while retaining little of the evidence needed to understand its failures or origins.

A compressed procedure may preserve a useful response but lose an exception. A source archive may preserve the exception but provide no practical route for discovering it in time.

The [creative-coverage vector](VECTOR-05-CREATIVE-COVERAGE.md) distinguishes those discovery bottlenecks. Retention needs the same care.

The [retention evidence note](VECTOR-08-RETENTION-EVIDENCE.md) examines specific mechanisms for preserving learned behavior. The [inheritance evidence note](VECTOR-08-INHERITANCE-EVIDENCE.md) examines contributions passed through continued training and structural development. Their primary results support narrower claims than comprehensive cognitive inheritance.

For our architecture, these are complementary responsibilities. Reusable organization should retain enough meaning to be applied and revised. Historical material should retain what its actual capture and preservation contract promises.

One responsibility cannot silently stand in for the other.


## Memory tiers should describe duties, not permanent anatomy

The earlier [memory-tier discussion](../../../../../components/substrate/docs/MEMORY-TIERS-AND-GRAPH-RETIREMENT.md) proposes bounded active cognition, reusable organized knowledge and historical/source retention. Each can contain several representations and internal layers.

That remains a useful functional distinction.

**Active organization** serves current cognition and action: working interpretations, task state, useful associations, immediate commitments and selected executable machinery. Its limited capacity gives specialization and retirement practical meaning.

**Reusable organization** keeps conditional expertise available without requiring continuous activation: procedures, schemas, models, relationship machinery, diagnostic cases and development methods. Access, loading and checking still cost something.

**Historical retention** preserves the earlier material promised by its capture contract. Historical investigation can construct a temporary view without making the old interpretation current again.

These duties can overlap in one world and be distributed across several systems. They do not prescribe one hot TRACE, one warm TRACE and one cold TL, or one device for each zone.

Source fidelity, epistemic status, current eligibility, physical residency and reuse cost are separate axes. A current finding can reside in cold storage. An active hypothesis can remain unresolved. A retired method can be historically valuable.

This lets us preserve your central preference: the working graph need not carry every discarded idea, procedure or participant.

## Retirement should remove the body and preserve only the remaining duty

Several operations can lead to a smaller active arrangement.

Deactivation withdraws active participation or default activation. Outstanding invocations and effects still need explicit continuation, cancellation or resolution. Eviction removes residency while leaving a capability eligible for reuse. Retirement ends its status as a current contribution. Historical access retrieves earlier material for a particular investigation. Reclamation removes retained material whose preservation duty has ended.

Their effects differ.

Suppose a procedure is retired because its old assumption no longer fits. Its body should leave ordinary current selection and traversal. Historical access can still study it if the necessary record remains available.

A delayed result, old index or pinned consumer can complicate serving behavior. Some compact status or exclusion information may be necessary while that condition persists. It can live outside cognitive traversal, and its storage and cleanup costs still count.

This does not justify keeping a permanent semantic vertex for every deletion. Minimum continuing control information should follow the actual serving or custody duty and be reclaimable when that duty clears.

Likewise, a historical note that something was deleted is useful only for what it actually tells us. It does not restore the deleted body or establish that the source remains available.

Your preference against active tombstones is compatible with this approach. The existing TRACE bakeoff and owning contracts still govern their concrete implementation; this essay does not select or replace them.

TL has no complete historical-reconstruction promise after reclamation. A cold immutable history would need its own explicit capture frontier, retention owner and recovery behavior. Immutability of captured material would still not establish that all relevant events were captured.

A later claim that a retired contribution can be reopened must therefore identify what remains accessible. If the necessary material is gone, reacquisition may be costly, partial or impossible.

## Retiring machinery does not end an unfinished responsibility

A historical body can leave the active graph while some effects of its work remain current.

A physical experiment might still be running. A resource might still be occupied. A callback might arrive after its originating worker has been replaced. A published conclusion might still depend on evidence that needs preservation.

Those are present responsibilities.

The successor arrangement should identify who continues them, what state is required and how uncertain effects will be resolved. Two descendants must not both assume an unfinished operation never happened and independently repeat it.

An appropriate custodian might retain a small amount of current operational state. That is different from keeping the retired participant's complete body active.

A split also does not duplicate a budget, device or reservation. A copied capability description does not grant another allocation.

Rights and responsibilities can be transferred or delegated under the existing authority. Copying learned behavior, an identifier or an ancestry record does not itself make that transfer.

This distinction need not create a human approval event for every internal update. Standing authority can cover ordinary development and reuse. Consequential changes remain governed by the actual commitments and granted powers.

The objective is continuity of the work with the smallest adequate retained organization.

## What earns continued retention?

Frequency of use is one useful signal. It is insufficient on its own.

A frequently used procedure may be cheap to reconstruct. A rarely used distinction may prevent a consequential misinterpretation. An expensive composite may provide a capability no simpler arrangement currently offers. An apparently obsolete method may become useful after the environment changes.

Retention can preserve immediate performance, future adaptation, investigation options or accountability. These benefits have different evidence and timescales.

I would evaluate a retention policy over a declared continuation horizon. Where outcomes and costs have been mapped to an explicit common measure, one accounting illustration is

\[
V_H(\pi)=
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H}\gamma^{t-1}U(o_t)\right]
-
\mathbb{E}\!\left[
C_{\mathrm{acquire}}+
C_{\mathrm{transform}}+
C_{\mathrm{maintain}}+
C_{\mathrm{access}}+
C_{\mathrm{reactivate}}+
C_{\mathrm{use}}+
C_{\mathrm{assess}}+
C_{\mathrm{repair}}
\right].
\]

Here \(\pi\) describes what is retained, in which form, for which eligible uses. The outcomes \(o_t\) concern later work, not merely successful reads. \(C_{\mathrm{use}}\) includes ordinary selection, inference and task execution. The costs cover the declared horizon without double counting; \(U\) excludes costs counted separately. \(U\) and the discount factor are explicit comparison choices.

This is an accounting aid, not a computable universal retention law. Quality, capability, attention, elapsed time and expenditure may need separate reporting. Future tasks and rare losses are uncertain.

Preservation and continuing obligations also constrain what a policy may discard. Those duties should not disappear because a local utility estimate is small.

Contributions are not generally additive. Two individually unhelpful records may enable a useful comparison. A controller, representation and calibration can work as a coupled arrangement. Retention decisions can concern those dependencies rather than independently ranking every atom.

The relevant question is what a retained arrangement buys relative to a capable alternative over the actual horizon.

“Contested” should therefore mean maintained organization earns its cost through useful work, adaptation or a continuing duty. It need not mean all experts optimize one popularity score.

## Selective loss can help without becoming a universal virtue

Removing obsolete distinctions can simplify choice. Consolidating duplicated mechanisms can reduce maintenance. Ending activation can free resources for other work.

These are plausible benefits of selective loss.

Loss can also remove a rare exception, a divergent method or the evidence needed to diagnose a later failure. A smaller representation can look more coherent because the material that challenged it has disappeared.

We should assess what remains usable after the loss, including changed demands. Compression ratio and average benchmark retention provide incomplete accounts.

A conditional schema can preserve a broad distinction while leaving domain-specific variation and source detail available elsewhere. It should not silently turn unknown conditions into an ordinary case. That carries forward your generalized-schema, specific-schema and delta idea without requiring one expression style for every expert.

Author diversity and different representations can be useful resources. Their value should be examined through complementary errors, new combinations and later work rather than equating all noise with creativity.

There is also a practical middle ground between keeping a full arrangement active and destroying it. A method can stop receiving default activation while selected evidence or a reconstruction recipe remains accessible under another budget.

That middle ground has costs. A locator, archived procedure and recovery path all consume resources. They should earn maintenance too.

Biological forgetting and death motivate questions about turnover and selective organization. Their particular implementation is not a requirement for an artificial system. Preserving reliable external memory may be a valuable advantage of our medium.

## Reconstruction is a capability with conditions

Reconstruction can mean recovering historical content, rebuilding a representation, reinstantiating a procedure or restoring a working capability.

Those are different endpoints.

To rebuild a representation, a successor may need the source versions, schema interpretation, transformation method and tool dependencies. To recover competence, it may also need training examples, a compatible model, assessment and renewed interaction.

Some operations are deterministic under retained inputs. Others are stochastic or depend on external state that cannot be recreated. Recovering an artifact need not recover its past behavior.

Reconstruction therefore needs an honest account of required material, availability, work and expected fidelity. “Can be rebuilt” should have a meaningful endpoint.

For instance, a procedural recipe can remain readable after its original library disappears while becoming substantially harder to execute. A preserved controller can still load while no longer interpreting the new sensor correctly.

The architecture should permit different preservation choices. A one-use exploratory draft may warrant little retention. A widely reused capability may warrant keeping a working implementation alongside its reconstruction method. A consequential historical claim may warrant exact evidence even after the associated procedure is retired.

We should also distinguish retrieving existing knowledge from learning a capability again. Historical reconstruction is not always cheap, but neither does every cold-memory read constitute renewed training.

No choice promises unlimited reversibility. Irreversible reclamation can be sensible where its consequences and actual duties are understood.

## The successor needs to notice inherited mismatch

Inheritance can reduce the effort needed to start while making old assumptions more persuasive.

A mature procedure comes with a history of success. A concise schema makes a familiar interpretation easy. A specialist relationship makes certain questions cheap to ask. Each can bias later work.

A successor should therefore inherit the ability to investigate mismatches as well as a favored response.

Some inherited mismatches produce no observable residual under familiar inputs. Independently varied cases or other evidence may be needed to reveal them; agreement with the predecessor is insufficient.

This can include known applicability conditions, informative cases, residual uncertainty, competing interpretations and methods for inspecting actual effects. It can also include a way to develop a new representation when the inherited one is inadequate.

A failure record is especially useful when it identifies a condition or diagnostic distinction. Repeating an old narrative about failure without its scope can become another inherited error.

Assessment cases can travel with a capability. They should remain evidence about particular behavior. A passed inherited suite does not establish fit to a new environment, and later tests should not merely repeat the demonstrations used to construct the contribution.

This suggests an important target: **inherit support for revision**. A descendant can gain from a mature capability while remaining able to change its interpretation, machinery or cooperation.

It is possible for a richer inheritance to reduce that freedom through excessive dependencies, opaque compression or expensive migration. That trade belongs in the evaluation.

Human understanding can benefit from retained examples, explanations and inspectable effects. Those aids are themselves fallible. Legibility and compatibility do not supply a domain verdict where the evaluator is missing.


## A hypothetical successor of the laboratory-hand world

Consider the laboratory-hand programme from [Vector 7](VECTOR-07-PROGRAMME-SEARCH.md). Its mechanical, control and measurement worlds develop a joint diagnostic distinction between command, observed contact and inferred load.

Through investigation, they acquire a procedure for identifying which uncertainty warrants another observation. A maintained composite supplies that procedure alongside a shared representation and conditional controller.

This is a hypothetical architecture case, not a qualified robot or selected design.

### Continued work on the existing apparatus

The programme first replaces a controller while continuing an existing experiment.

The successor needs the relevant current apparatus state, known uncertainties, pending results and current resource custody. It can inherit a procedure without assuming the new controller has already enacted it.

A command sent before replacement may still have physical consequences. A delayed observation needs to be interpreted against the operation and conditions that produced it.

Some history can leave the working graph. The state needed to understand those continuing effects remains current until the work is resolved or deliberately transferred.

This is continuity of operation. It differs from launching a new instance with a clean task.

### A new hand in another laboratory

A second laboratory wants the capability on another specimen with different tendon routing and sensors.

The transmitted contribution can include the shared diagnostic representation, controller implementation, useful observations, conditions from earlier assessments and a calibration procedure.

The first hand's numerical calibration is evidence about that specimen. It is not established calibration for the second.

The descendant should begin with a useful prior and investigate the differences. It may find that the old controller remains suitable after calibration, that another representation is needed, or that physical changes alter the achievable behavior.

Copying the recipe does not copy apparatus, measurement access or the earlier laboratory's permission to act. Required resources and responsibility remain explicit.

The useful inherited advantage is reduced effort in reaching supported capability on the new specimen, with any improvement in noticing mismatch also counted. We need not demand identical behavior or identical internal organization.

### A different cooperation develops

Repeated observations suggest that the new controller cannot distinguish sensor lag from a particular contact change through its existing state.

The descendant investigates. Its measurement and control worlds develop a new joint estimator and observation policy. Part of the earlier coordination becomes unnecessary.

This is development of inherited organization. It changes the representation and relationship that support action, rather than merely copying a successful topology.

The descendant may retain the new arrangement as a composite, move some machinery into a shared tool, or keep separate worlds with a useful cooperation. Those choices remain open.

If a later split separates the measurement and control worlds, each needs the relevant machinery or access to the joint capability. Naming two successors cannot divide the estimator automatically.

A further caller might inherit a procedure for developing such an estimator rather than this specimen's estimator itself. That stronger claim needs evidence under changed conditions.

### A retired branch becomes relevant again

Earlier, the programme retired a mechanical alternative because it was unsuitable under the old conditions. Its body left ordinary traversal.

The new specimen introduces a different contact regime. A question now makes that alternative worth investigating.

A historical agent can search the retained programme records within its budget. If the design, observations and rejection conditions remain available, it can reconstruct the earlier rationale and assess the changed case.

That investigation does not silently reinstate the retired conclusion as current. The old rejection may still hold, or its relevant condition may have changed.

If only a deletion note remains, the agent should report that limit. A familiar name or pointer cannot substitute for missing observations.

The comparison now includes historical discovery, reconstruction, new measurement and adaptation. Reopening can be useful without being free.

### What the example discriminates

A snapshot explains immediate continuation. A calibration method explains some transfer. A developed estimator explains adaptation. Historical access explains one possible route for reconsidering a discarded approach.

These are different mechanisms and benefits.

A system that preserves all files but repeatedly reconstructs the diagnostic capability has retained history with limited inheritance. A system that copies the old controller cheaply while missing its mismatch has inherited machinery with poor adaptation.

A useful developmental inheritance would make the descendant more capable of reaching, assessing and revising an appropriate arrangement. It can retain that advantage while changing its structure substantially.

## Changing a software model can be a substantial inheritance event too

The physical example makes specimen differences visible. Software compatibility can be less visible while remaining consequential.

A replacement model can interpret the same compact schema differently. An executable dependency can change behavior under the same procedure name. A shared representation can lose an exception during translation.

Artifact identity and syntactic validity do not establish semantic continuity.

Some contributions can transfer directly. Others need translation, renewed assessment or relearning. A maintained composite may be easier to reuse than dismantling it into supposedly independent skills.

External memory and frozen-model reuse remain useful options. Developing a richer TRACE representation does not imply that the model's weights have changed. Training on that representation is a further intervention with its own acquisition and transfer questions.

Your hypothesis about generalized schemas, specialized schemas and deltas can therefore be tested at several points: navigation, reasoning, executable construction, model adaptation and subsequent development. Success at one point should not stand in for all the others.

A portable representation should help preserve consequential distinctions while allowing domain meanings and expression to remain diverse. We should not make one model's favored shorthand the compulsory language of every descendant.

## Population development is a later extension with different claims

Once contributions can be retained, transformed and assessed, a population could explore alternative organizations. Different descendants can acquire different methods, cooperate, exchange contributions or form composites.

That is a possible extension of the machinery. It is not a prerequisite for useful inheritance.

The [inheritance evidence note](VECTOR-08-INHERITANCE-EVIDENCE.md) distinguishes particular precedents for transferring acquired training state and evolving structure or environments. These precedents do not establish open-ended cognitive development or a universal fitness measure.

Selection also needs a declared unit and assessment. Choosing an individual policy, a relationship, a composite or a shared environment can produce different pressures.

A locally successful participant might consume a scarce resource needed by others. A useful shared method may have no single beneficiary that pays its full maintenance cost. A composite may perform well only with a particular surrounding facility.

Those interactions mean we cannot infer society-wide improvement from a rising score assigned to each participant. Nor should ancestral success give a descendant permanent priority.

Competition and cooperation can be useful mechanisms where they create better work. Their form should follow the environment and evidence.

The nearer commitment is simpler: the architecture should permit contributions to cross a transformation with meaningful scope, actual dependencies, selective retention and observable consequences. Evolutionary search can later use that surface without requiring large rewrites.

## Inheritance should increase autonomy at the right level

The human and the surrounding society are part of this continuation.

A useful system can retain which clarifications changed consequential decisions, which routine work proceeded successfully under standing direction and which investigations produced little value. It can use those patterns to prepare better alternatives and undertake more of the work independently.

This is a capability target for the wider collaboration, not a requirement to confine cognition to one human and one assistant.

Inference about a person's preferences remains an interpretation. A behavioral pattern is not automatically a governing objective or permission. The same distinction applies to inherited operating practices: a previously successful coordination method can be useful without becoming a permanent rule.

The architecture can help reduce repeated explanation and housekeeping while keeping consequential choices connected to actual intent. It should also be able to notice when its inherited model of the work no longer fits.

Human attention belongs in the cost account. A descendant that performs more tasks but repeatedly requires the user to reconstruct its assumptions can have limited practical value.

The desired trajectory is greater verified capability across the society, with the human spending attention where their contribution changes direction, meaning or an important choice.


## The comparison must let simpler systems inherit too

A strong simpler arrangement can already retain procedures, model checkpoints, source access, test cases, authored tools and shared expertise. It can continue learning, invent methods and change its organization where permitted.

That is the relevant baseline.

Requiring the baseline to restart from nothing would confound inheritance with unequal access to earlier work. Giving only ANIMA a stronger successor model, richer evaluator or prepared environment would introduce further confounds.

Several focused comparisons could separate the mechanisms.

**Retained evidence versus retained capability.** Give both arrangements comparable source access. Examine whether an explicit conditional procedure or developed relationship makes later work more achievable or less costly than recovering relevant material and reconstructing a method.

**Snapshot versus developmental contribution.** Compare a mature starting arrangement with a recipe or adaptation rule under changed conditions. Examine usable capability, development costs and subsequent revision. Count the preparation of both contributions.

**Coupled inheritance versus independent artifacts.** Where competence may reside in a relationship, compare preserving the arrangement with retaining its separate constituents. Keep actual shared dependencies available where promised. Investigate which contribution explains any difference.

**Selective retention versus extensive retention.** Under a declared active and preservation budget, examine choice quality, missed exceptions, useful unfamiliar combinations, maintenance and reactivation. Extensive retention should not receive unbounded resources, and selective retention should not conceal its reconstruction work.

**Continuation under a changed model or body.** Examine how inherited meaning and behavior survive new implementations and physical conditions. Separate a generally stronger successor from a useful transferred contribution.

These are discriminating questions, not an experiment launched by this essay or a mandatory five-part campaign.

The continuation horizon should include familiar tasks, altered conditions and demands not supplied during contribution discovery. A planted exception can diagnose a mechanism; realistic changed work is needed to assess broader usefulness.

Report actual completed outcomes, losses, incomplete work, applicability errors and repair. Include acquisition, unsuccessful discovery, transformation, source preservation, access, training, assessment, maintenance, recalibration and attention.

Shared acquisition should be charged once where it occurred. Descendant-specific preparation and continued service costs should be charged where incurred. A cost shifted into a background world remains a cost.

A diagnostic intervention can identify why inheritance failed. Supplying the missing source, correct translation or decisive calibration is different evidence from autonomous discovery of that need.

A surviving artifact is not the endpoint. The endpoint concerns what further work becomes possible, reliable or cheaper because of what was retained.

## What would constitute a deeper advance?

Checkpoint continuation, procedural reuse, external memory, learned optimizers and structural evolution already have substantial precedents. Their combination is a research opportunity, but combination alone does not establish a breakthrough.

The stronger ANIMA hypothesis concerns cumulative development across interacting worlds.

Experience could improve the distinctions used to notice problems, the operations available to solve them, the cooperation supporting those operations and the environment in which later capability develops. Selected contributions could then help descendants adapt to different demands.

The relevant continuity would be retained developmental advantage.

A descendant might inherit less internal machinery while becoming better at constructing an appropriate arrangement. Another might preserve a mature composite because its working expertise remains valuable. Several might share a facility whose improved methods benefit all of them.

This permits progress without one ever-expanding active graph or one model that must contain the whole society.

It also leaves difficult questions open: how useful abstractions are discovered, how assessment remains informative, how tail knowledge survives, how representation meanings transfer, how maintenance costs grow, and when integration helps more than independence.

A bounded result could establish that one developed contribution transfers usefully through a changed participant or requirement. Stronger claims need wider continuations, independent assessment and complete cost accounting.

## What I would carry forward from Vector 8

I propose five commitments for your later consideration.

1. **Retain conditional capability and support for revision.** A successor can receive working machinery, developmental methods, accessible evidence or a combination, with their scopes distinguishable.
2. **Keep active organization selective.** Retired bodies leave ordinary traversal; current responsibilities and necessary serving information retain only the state their duties require.
3. **Make historical availability an explicit responsibility.** A pointer, checkpoint or immutable captured record does not establish complete recoverability.
4. **Let inheritance include relationships and environments.** Contributions can be coupled, shared or transformed; descendants need not preserve the ancestor's exact organization.
5. **Assess cumulative advantage against capable continuations.** Count losses and complete costs while allowing the alternatives to reuse, adapt and invent too.

The current [responsibility map](../../RESPONSIBILITIES.md) supplies the ownership boundary. Worlds retain domain interpretation, state custody and exported obligations. The broad substrate supports hosting, lifecycle, resources and controlled organizational change. TRACE contributes its granted interpretation and episode duties; TL contributes attributable source organization and retrieval within its own retention scope. SST supplies bounded exploration. STC supplies software construction and realization.

No single project should silently acquire all inheritance, archive, learning and evaluation duties through this vocabulary.

The most useful near-term design commitment is a meaningful path from experience to a reusable contribution, through later use and revision, with selective retirement and honest recovery limits. A universal genotype, fixed brain anatomy, population fitness function or permanent hierarchy can remain open.
