## Research map

The user's preferred priority is A → C → B → D, not a rigid stage sequence. A asks whether organized knowledge can directly participate in computation and enable transferable useful operations; C asks whether models can learn and use schemas/operations beyond equally informative teaching; B asks whether experience can yield useful new representations, procedures, relationships, and cumulative discovery; D asks whether computational and physical organization should co-develop under measured resource limits. The user wants meaningful intermediate work, welcomes intelligence and improvement, and prefers solid foundations before the stronger B frontier while allowing earlier hardware-enabling research.

## Active planned comparisons

- **E1:** module-owned bounded requirement/proposal exchange versus competent central coordination; include an internally resolvable conflict, a genuine incompatibility to escalate, and a changed requirement. Measure verified integration, central traffic, attention, context, cost, rework, and handoff reconstruction.
- **E2:** conditional schemas/procedures or executable operations versus capable raw evidence/tools and equally informative curated text; assess fresh-task composition, exception handling, changed conditions, and complete acquisition/maintenance/task cost.
- **E3:** prepare a discriminating schema-learning protocol; distinguish supplied definitions from learned use, disclose actual training/supplied material, test new compositions and drift, and track cost. Training remains separately scoped.
- **E4:** compare a competent fixed workflow, conditional text/procedure memory, and candidate feedback-driven selection/revision on a fresh task and changed condition. Include failed episodes, explicit correction, comparative advantage, and pre-outcome predictions when testing prediction correction.

Record hypotheses, tasks/inputs, candidates/evaluators, supplied versus discovered machinery, assistance and interventions, costs, failures/timeouts, prospective criteria, and observed results. Positive, negative, and inconclusive results can resolve a research question within its envelope. A negative experiment cannot close an unimplemented product obligation. Novelty and broad superiority are not established.
